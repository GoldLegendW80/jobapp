# Spec: opportunities

Module of [CAPABILITY-MAP.md](CAPABILITY-MAP.md), lane product (#353). Depends on `jobs` (the postings the sweep fills, `set_status`, the add path), `profile` (the `search` section), `apply` (the Like of A12, the queue, Full auto), `ingestion` (the import of onboarding step 1), `documents` (the CV upload panel, the writers) and `browser` (the browser sources of B8 to B10 and the sign in hand off of B12). Plan: [tasks/opportunities/todo.md](tasks/opportunities/todo.md). Issues: the spec #72, the swipe deck #74, freelance and project platforms #75, the first login wizard #76, simple by default #77, the rename #73. Intent: `docs/intent/swipe-onboarding-opportunities.md` of the owner's job workspace (outside this repository), requested and decided on 2026-10-10.

## Objective

The app shows what it found one card at a time, and one gesture decides: a Like starts the CV, the letter and the application, a Pass hides the posting for good. A first login wizard asks what the person is looking for, in plain words, and every page shows the essentials first with the rest behind "Advanced". Postings are no longer only jobs: freelance missions from the platforms that allow it come in through the same sweep, and at the end of the lane the whole app calls a posting an **Opportunity** (table, API, code, specs, UI).

User stories:
- As a person, I open **Discover** and see one card: title, company or client, location or remote, contract type, pay when known, and why it matches me. I swipe right (or press Like) and the app writes my documents and starts the application; I swipe left (or press Pass) and I never see that posting again. I can undo my last swipe.
- As a person in Review first (the default), every Like lands in the review queue ready for my Approve; in Full auto, a Like is the click, within the guardrails of apply.
- As a new person, at my first sign in a wizard of seven short steps fills my profile from my CV or LinkedIn export, asks what I look for, where, which contracts, my pay, how the app should apply, and shows a summary. I can skip what is not essential and change everything later.
- As a freelancer, I pick the freelance and project sources I want swept, the same way as the job boards, and the missions they list arrive in my deck.
- As anyone, every settings page shows a few plain options first and keeps the rest under one "Advanced" section.

## The swipe deck (#74)

**What is in the deck.** The person's postings with status `found` or `shortlisted`, no live or ended application, fit at or above "Show postings from" (`search.min_fit`, default 70) or no fit yet when the posting has no text, not matched by a `never_apply` rule (the sweep marks those `skipped`; the deck checks again with apply's folded matcher, `apply.guardrails.never_apply`, so a Cybersecurity title the sweep's whole word match missed never shows). Order: fit, highest first, then the newest `found_on`, then the id (a stable order, so a reload shows the same card). A filter row, collapsed under "Advanced": contract types, remote or on site, source. An empty deck says why ("Nothing new above your threshold of 70. Find new postings, or lower the threshold in Search options.") with the two links.

**The card.** Title; company or client; location, or "Remote" / "Hybrid" when known; contract type in plain words (Permanent, Fixed term, VIE, Internship, Apprenticeship, Freelance mission); pay when known ("45,000 to 55,000 EUR a year", "600 EUR a day"), never a guess (new nullable columns of the posting, filled from what an ATS API states or what the posting's text states in a shape an allow list accepts, task O3; one pay per posting: when a text states a salary and a daily rate, the one that fits the contract type is kept; the person can edit or remove it, and a later sweep or rescore keeps that edit); the fit badge; the match reason: up to three competencies the posting asks that the profile has, and the first gap ("Matches: Python, LLM evaluation, FastAPI. Gap: Kubernetes."), from the stored `competencies` and `gaps`; the source ("via Greenhouse", "via Remote OK" with its link, which some sources require); a "More" that opens the posting's text in a sheet. No dashes as separators in any of it.

**The gestures.** Drag right past a threshold, the Like button, or the right arrow key: a Like. Drag left, the Pass button, or the left arrow key: a Pass. The card follows the pointer and springs back under the threshold; with reduced motion, no animation, buttons only. One card in flight at a time: the next gesture waits for the server's answer of the previous one.

**A Like** calls apply's `POST /api/postings/{id}/like` (A12a, PR #428: `apply.on_like`, the contract with this deck; its signature and answers change only with both plans). 201: the card leaves, a toast says "Liked. Your documents are being written." in Review first, or "Liked. The app will apply after the hold unless you cancel." in Full auto, with Undo. 409 `{detail, code, application_id}`: the card stays, the sentence of the code is shown (never apply, the same role already under way, already applied, closed posting, ...), and "Pass" is offered. 404: the card leaves (another tab deleted it).

**A Pass** is `POST /api/postings/{id}/pass`: the posting moves to `skipped` through the jobs module's `set_status` under its lock, with a `status` event whose data holds `{via: "swipe", from}`. A skipped posting never comes back: the sweep's duplicate rule (same URL, or same company and title) finds the row and adds nothing, and the deck never shows `skipped`. The Pass is kept as a signal (the event) for later matching; nothing reads it now, and a later separate task may use it (decision 10).

**Undo** acts on the person's last swipe only, while the toast shows (10 seconds) or from the "Undo last" button until the next swipe:
- a Pass: `DELETE /api/postings/{id}/pass` puts back the status in the event's `from` when the posting is still `skipped` by that same event (any later event, a status changed elsewhere: 409 `changed`, nothing undone);
- a Like: apply's `POST /api/applications/{id}/cancel` (A6) while the application has not reached `submitting`; documents already asked are kept (they cost nothing more to keep and belong to the posting). In Full auto the hold of at least 15 minutes means a Like can always be undone before any click.

**Isolation and limits.** Every route acts on the caller's rows only (404 otherwise). The deck answers at most 20 cards per call, with a cursor. Swipes are idempotent: a second Pass of the same posting answers 200 with the same state; a second Like answers apply's 409 `in_progress` with the application id.

## Freelance and project platforms as sources (#75)

**The model.** A person's `search` section gets a list `feeds`, beside `boards` (the ATS boards of the sweep) and `browser_sources` (browser B8 to B10): each entry `{kind, terms (optional include terms on top of the person's title terms), enabled}`. The sweep runs every enabled feed after the boards, with the same rules as a board: the person's include, exclude and location terms, the days window, the `never_apply` rules, the duplicate rule, the score, status `found`. A posting gets `contract_type = Freelance` when the source says it is a contract or freelance mission, else the contract type the posting text states (the jobs module's existing rule). The source is recorded (`source` column, for instance `remoteok`), with the posting's own address on the source, so the card links back to it. As built (O7, 2026-10-20): a feed's own `terms`, when set, must also match the title; a feed's posting is a duplicate of a posting with the same address, or with the same company and title whatever its location says (one row naming every source, for instance `ashby:acme, remoteok`), except for the placeholder company of a client the feed does not name ("Client on Hacker News"), which only its address makes a duplicate; a posting the person's never apply rules match is stored `skipped` with the rule as its note and the `never_apply` events, exactly as a board's, with the same folded match apply uses (`app/jobs/never_apply.py`, since #601), so "Cybersecurity", "Cybersécurité" or a soft hyphen inside "Security" are skipped too, and the deck checks again; the sources are `remoteok`, `weworkremotely`, `hackernews:freelance` and `hackernews:hiring`; in the "Freelancer? Seeking freelancer?" thread only the posts of clients ("SEEKING FREELANCER") are missions, the posts of freelancers offering their work ("SEEKING WORK") are skipped; a feed of more than 1,000 items is reported `too_many`, counted before any item is parsed, and nothing of it is kept; HTML is made plain in time linear in the text, off the sweep's event loop; a Hacker News item is read up to 256 KB, a comment answered 429 stops its feed (`rate_limited`), and no request of a feed starts past the sweep's time limit (`time_budget`); a test stack without the fake feeds server reads no feed (`stub_missing`); a feed's text is kept up to 20,000 characters. A feed's failure is reported in the sweep's `sources` with its code, and the sweep goes on.

**Free, and within the platform's terms or with the person's own opt in.** Checked on 2026-10-20 by reading each platform's robots file, terms and API pages (the claims and their sources are in the table). The catalogue in code holds two lists: the supported kinds, read by the sweep with plain HTTP, and the platforms read with Playwright after the person's opt in (decision 12, task O7b). The Search options page shows both, each with its reason in one line and, for the second, the risk sentence, so the person knows what a switch means. A platform's terms change: each fetcher keeps the date and the address of the terms it was built against in its module, and the plan's measurements are redone when a task touches it. As built (O8, 2026-10-21): the Search options tab's card "Freelance and project sources" shows each supported feed with its switch and its attribution line, its optional words under Advanced, and the list "Read only with your opt in" with each platform's one line reason, the date of the check and a link to its terms, with no switch: O7b places its switches and the risk sentence under this card. "Add a mission by hand" opens the jobs module's Add a posting dialog with the contract Freelance preselected. A catalogue that cannot be read leaves the switches already saved on show. A sweep's summary lists the feeds under their own heading, with plain words for each code a feed reports and the number of postings the never apply rules set aside (`skipped_by_rule` per source in `GET /api/jobs/sweeps/{id}`); the posting page says "via Remote OK" (or the feed that found it) and links back to the posting there.

## The first login wizard (#76)

**When.** At the first sign in of an account whose profile is empty, the app opens `/welcome` instead of the home page. "Skip for now" records `preferences.onboarding.dismissed_at` and the home page shows a small card "Finish setting up" until the wizard is done. Every later sign in goes to the home page. The owner's account and every account with a profile already filled never see the wizard by itself (open question 4); `/welcome` stays reachable from Account.

**The steps.** A progress bar with the step names, Back and Next, "Skip this step" on every step but 2 and 7, each step saved when Next is pressed (through the profile's own `PUT /api/profile/{section}` with its revision, so a second tab gets the profile's 409), and each step passed (Next or "Skip this step") kept in the profile's `preferences.onboarding.steps_passed` through `POST /api/opportunities/onboarding/steps` (owner, 2026-10-20: written only by the wizard's routes, kept by a profile save and an import, never proposable by ingestion, never shown to a model; a step passed is no revision of the profile, so a second tab editing the profile is not sent a 409 by it, and it records nothing once the wizard is finished, #484), so a closed tab or another device resumes at the first step neither done nor passed. The summary shows what was saved, never what was typed then skipped; a step after the roles is never opened while no role is saved:
1. **Your profile.** Upload a CV (PDF or DOCX) or a LinkedIn data export (ZIP), or paste text: ingestion's upload (`POST /api/ingestion/documents`), its consent the first time, its estimate and its run (`POST /api/ingestion/runs`), all inside the step (ingestion's `RunStart` and `RunProgress`, #484), with "Review the proposals" opening `/profile/fill` at its review step; a CV also becomes a base CV through documents' upload panel (`upload-panel.tsx` of D24, which was built for this wizard to embed). The run goes on in the background while the next steps are filled. Skippable.
2. **What you are looking for.** Roles (title include terms, at least one), seniority, industries, company types. Essential.
3. **Where.** Locations (at least one, or "Anywhere"), and remote, hybrid or on site (`search.remote_stance`).
4. **How you want to work.** Several of: Permanent, Fixed term, Internship, Apprenticeship, VIE, Freelance mission (`search.contract_types`). Choosing Freelance mission shows the supported freelance sources with their switches (on by default for the person who chose freelance) and the line about the platforms that are not supported.
5. **Pay.** Optional: a yearly salary range with its currency, a daily rate with its currency, or "Rather not say". Shown on cards next to the posting's pay; never sent in a form (salary answers stay apply's `salary_stance`).
6. **How the app applies.** Review first (selected, "You approve every application") or Full auto ("A Like is enough. The app applies after a hold of at least 15 minutes, only above your threshold"), with apply's own settings route (`PUT /api/apply/settings`); Full auto is shown but disabled with "Available soon" until apply A13a has merged, and choosing it goes through A13b's settings card and its rehearsals, never around them. Then the language of documents (Automatic from the posting, English, French) and the tone (Plain, Formal, Warm).
7. **Summary.** Every answer in plain words with "Change" per step, the counts the first sweep would cover ("12 boards and 2 freelance sources"), and "Start": it records `preferences.onboarding.completed_at`, starts a first sweep (the jobs module's one start, `sweep.begin`, the same as `POST /api/jobs/sweeps`) and opens Discover. Under the answers, **the starter list** (O5b, issue #482, owner 2026-10-20): from the roles, industries, places and company types saved, public company boards (Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever), and always, to everyone whatever they saved (the person unticks what they do not want), the browser sources Google Careers and Business France VIE: both are searched for the person's first role, Google Careers in the person's first place when one is given, and when no role words are given they search every job and the entry says so ("Searches every job (no role given)"), from a curated catalog versioned in the repository (`api/app/opportunities/starter_catalog.json`: public company ATS identifiers only, each board asked once through the profile's board check before it ships, `starter_check.py`). Nothing is ticked and nothing is added until the person chooses: "Add all", or tick some and "Add the ticked ones"; each entry has "Remove". What is added goes to Search options enabled; what is added or removed is kept in `preferences.onboarding.starter_handled` (written only by `POST /api/opportunities/starter`) and never proposed again once gone from Search options. Never a board whose company matches one of the person's never apply rules or names cybersecurity, never LinkedIn nor a careers page. No target gives a small general list of boards; targets the catalog knows nothing of propose no board (the two sources stay), say which words, and point to Search options to add a board by its address. The same list, same routes and rules, is also offered in Search options as "Suggested boards and sites" (O5c, issue #497, owner 2026-10-20), for an account that skipped the wizard or wants more boards later: closed until opened unless the search has no board and no site, waiting while the tab has unsaved changes, and reloading the tab's forms after a choice.

**The new profile fields** (all optional, validated, exported and imported, shown in the profile's tabs too):
- in `search`: `seniority` (a list of `intern`, `junior`, `mid`, `senior`, `lead`, `principal`), `industries` (terms), `company_types` (a list of `startup`, `scaleup`, `large_company`, `public_sector`, `nonprofit`, `agency`, `consulting`), `compensation` (`{salary: {min, max, currency} | null, daily_rate: {min, max, currency} | null}`, whole numbers, currency an ISO 4217 code), `feeds` (above);
- a new section `preferences`: `onboarding: {completed_at, dismissed_at}`, `documents_language` (`auto`, `en`, `fr`; `auto` reads the posting's language as documents do today), `documents_tone` (`plain`, `formal`, `warm`; the documents writers read it, a documents lane follow up);
- application behaviour is apply's `apply_settings.mode_after_like` (A11), not a profile field, so there is one truth.

Seniority, industries and company types feed the score (decision 11) and never filter the sweep: a posting that matches them gets a bonus in the jobs scoring (`api/app/jobs/scoring.py`), a capped one, so the keyword model over skills stays the main part of the fit and a posting that matches none of them is still found and shown. The deck's filter uses contract types and remote stance. They are shown on the summary too. Compensation does not change the score.

## Simple by default (#77)

- **One shared component** `Advanced` (`web/src/components/advanced-section.tsx`): a section titled "Advanced", collapsed by default, its state kept per page in the browser (local storage wrapped in try and catch, so a blocked storage only means it starts collapsed), keyboard and screen reader friendly (a disclosure button with `aria-expanded`). Documents D13 created the first "Advanced" of the Search tab; it moves onto this component.
- **What goes up front:** at most five options per card, each with a default that works; everything numeric (thresholds, ceilings, hold lengths, pacing), regular expressions, per board exclude terms and debug or measurement details go under Advanced.
- **Plain labels.** No jargon in a label: "Fit score" stays (it is the app's own word) but "ATS" becomes "Application site", "sweep" becomes "Find new postings", "lease" never appears, "never apply rules" becomes "Roles to never apply for", contract codes read as words (CDI is "Permanent"). The word list lives in `web/src/lib/wording.ts`, one place, with a Vitest that every label of the listed pages comes from it or is plain English.
- **The pass, page by page:** each page's lane makes its own page simple, in its own files, with the shared component; this lane does Discover, the wizard and the opportunities pages, and opens one issue per other page with the list of what moves under Advanced.

## The rename (#73), last, in a freeze window

**What is renamed.** Every identifier, path and word that says job: the tables `jobs`, `job_events`, `job_files` and their enums (`job_status`, `job_event_kind`, ...), columns named `job_id`, indexes and constraints, the Python package `api/app/jobs/` (into `api/app/opportunities/`, beside this lane's code), its classes (`Job`, `NewJob`, `JobBusyError`, `lock_job`, ...), the routes `/api/jobs/...` (to `/api/opportunities/...`), the web feature `web/src/features/jobs/` and its routes `/jobs`, `/jobs/:id`, `/jobs/map`, `/jobs/:id/documents/:kind`, every UI string, the e2e specs, the deploy command `./deploy.sh jobs import`, `SPEC-jobs.md` (folded into this spec) and every mention in the other specs, plans, `CLAUDE.md`, `CAPABILITY-MAP.md`, `README.md` and `docs/`. Names that already say `posting` stay as they are: they were chosen neutral for this rename (intent, "Consequences"), so `posting_id` in apply, mail and documents is kept (planner default, open question 2).

**The data.** One migration renames in place (`ALTER TABLE ... RENAME`, `ALTER TYPE ... RENAME`, `ALTER INDEX ... RENAME`): metadata only in Postgres, every row kept, no copy; its `downgrade` renames back. A backup first (`ops/remote.sh backup`), a count of rows per table before and after in the deploy's verify figures. Redis keys and arq queue names with `job` in them are renamed with a drain first (no sweep or apply run open during the deploy).

**Old addresses keep working for a while.** The api answers every old `/api/jobs/...` path with 308 to the new one (method and body kept), for 30 days or two releases, whichever is later (planner default, open question 3), then a contract task removes them. The web keeps redirect routes from `/jobs...` to `/opportunities...` (query string kept, so bookmarked filters still open) for good: they cost nothing. Links stored in data (events, notifications, mail drafts) are rewritten by the migration when they hold an app path.

**The freeze.** Its start needs the find, apply and swipe tasks merged (owner, 2026-10-20). The planner reads that as: jobs and browser sources B8, B9 and B10, this lane's freelance sources O7 and O7b, apply A12, A13a and A13b, and the deck O2 (open question 1). Then: the captain announces the freeze on #354 and every lane issue; every lane merges what is approved and parks the rest (open pull requests stay open, no new push); the rename's slices are built on one stacked branch by one session, reviewed, and shipped as one batch by `ops/ship.sh`; the captain announces the end with the rebase script, and each parked pull request is rebased with it (`ops/rename-rebase.sh`, a mapping of old paths and names to new ones, run on a branch, its result reviewed). Mail (paused) and every task planned after the freeze is written against the new names.

## Testing strategy

- Unit: the deck's query and order (fit ties, no fit, the `never_apply` fold over the 28 titles of apply A2's table), the card text (pay ranges, contract words, the match reason from competencies and gaps), each feed's parser against recorded fixtures (written from the public pages on the task's start date, no copy of their scripts, invented companies), the freelance tag rules (accept and refuse tables), the new profile fields (valid, missing, wrong type, oversize, currency codes), the wording list.
- Integration (real Postgres and Redis): every route with two people A and B (B's posting answers 404 to A's Pass, undo and deck), the Pass under the jobs lock racing a sweep and a Like, the undo after a later status change, a sweep against a fake feeds server (`tests/feeds_stub.py`: ok, 404, 429, timeout, malformed, 5,000 items), the wizard's saves with stale revisions.
- Journeys (Playwright, on the Mac stack, `e2e-*@example.org` accounts only, the fake ATS forms and fake feeds only): swipe through a deck, Like into the queue, Pass, undo each; the wizard from an empty account to the first sweep; freelance sources switched on and their missions in the deck; the rename's redirects of old addresses.
- No test reads a real platform or applies to a real company.

## Boundaries

- **Always:** a Like goes through `apply.on_like`; a Pass through `set_status`; a source is read only when it is free and either its terms allow it or the person opted in to the risk of that platform (O7b), with its attribution shown; user scoping on every query.
- **Ask first:** a new source not in the table above; asking a platform for an API key or written permission; letting a Pass change scores (a separate later task, decision 10); any change to apply's routes.
- **Never:** read a platform whose terms forbid automated use for a person who has not accepted its risk sentence of the current version, or at more than a person's pace; sign in to a platform on the person's behalf (the person signs in himself, by the hand off of B12); pay for a source or an alert; send an outreach e-mail by itself in Review first, or at all while mail sending is paused; show or apply to a cybersecurity role.

## Success criteria

1. On the Jetson, the owner opens Discover on his phone, Likes three postings and Passes three; the Likes are in the review queue with their documents being written, the Passes never come back after the next sweep, and one undo of each works.
2. A new invited account goes through the wizard from an empty profile to its first sweep without touching JSON, and finds its answers in the profile.
3. A person who switches on Remote OK, We Work Remotely and the Hacker News freelance thread finds their missions in the deck, each card naming and linking its source; a person who accepts the risk sentence of one opt in platform finds, from his own signed in session, the missions that platform shows him.
4. Every settings page of the app shows its essentials first and the rest under one Advanced section, with no jargon in labels.
5. After the freeze, nothing in the code, the API, the UI or the specs says job, every old address still opens the right page, and every row is where it was.
6. No person can see or swipe another's postings (automated isolation tests).
