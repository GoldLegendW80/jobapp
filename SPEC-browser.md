# Spec: browser

Module of [CAPABILITY-MAP.md](CAPABILITY-MAP.md). Depends on `platform`; used by `apply` and by the jobs sweep's website search. Umbrella issue: #67. Plan: [tasks/browser/todo.md](tasks/browser/todo.md). The owner's decisions of 2026-10-14 are taken (below); nothing is built.

## Objective

Each person of the app has one real browser on the Jetson, with their own logins, which an agent can drive and the person can watch live from any device and take over at any moment: to solve a captcha, to sign in to LinkedIn, Google or an applicant tracking system, or to finish a form by hand. The same browser service also reads the job sources that have no API (LinkedIn, Google Careers, company career sites, Business France VIE). It is the Jetson's version of what the command line does on the owner's Mac today: a real Chrome with a persistent profile, driven over CDP, headed, human paced.

User stories:
- As a person, I open "Your browser" in Account and see my browser live; I sign in to the sites I use once, and I stay signed in next time.
- As a person, while the agent fills an application I watch it in the job page, and I click "Take over" to type myself; "Give back" lets the agent continue.
- As a person, I am told when my browser needs me ("A captcha needs you on Example Robotics"), and the link opens the live view on that tab.
- As a person, I click "Forget all logins" and my browser starts empty next time.
- As a person, when the agent reaches a site that needs an account, I am told, I sign in or sign up myself in the live view, I click "I'm signed in", and the agent goes on where it was; the next time, the site already knows me.
- As a person, I ask the agent to complete my profile on a site I use (Workday, LinkedIn, a company portal) from the profile of the app (SPEC-apply, "Complete my profile on a site").
- As the owner, I know that one person can never see, drive or read another person's browser.

## Owner decisions (taken on 2026-10-14)

1. **Accounts on job sites: the person signs in, the browser profile remembers.** The owner's words: "The agent open playwright, user sign in or log in, we save all the playwright profile per user so next opening will be connected. User can ask to complete his profile on the website with all informations." Hence: the app never chooses a password and never creates an account by itself. When a site needs an account, the agent opens the page in the person's browser session on the Jetson, and the person signs in or signs up himself in the live view (the sign in hand off, below). The browser profile of each person (cookies, local storage) is persisted on the Jetson, so the next run is already connected. A new action, "Complete my profile on this site", lets the person ask the agent to fill the candidate profile of a site from the app's profile (SPEC-apply), with the guardrails of a form fill.
2. **LinkedIn: full use.** The owner's words: "Full use". The app searches LinkedIn for jobs and submits Easy Apply from the person's own signed in session, in Review first and in Full auto too. Kept: human pacing, a low request rate (10 to 30 seconds per page; one Easy Apply every 10 minutes and 15 a day at most; the search's one sweep a day and 5 result pages were removed by the owner on 2026-10-28, no daily limit and no page cap) and the per company and per day guards of the apply module. LinkedIn stays a per person switch, off by default, since the app has several users. Turning it on shows one plain sentence, which the person accepts once: "LinkedIn's terms forbid automated use of its site, and your account may be limited or closed; the app keeps to the pace of a person, but the risk is yours." The acceptance is recorded with its date and the version of the sentence, and without it no LinkedIn work starts (search, Easy Apply, profile).

## Which browser runs on the Jetson

- **Chromium for Linux arm64, headed, on Xvfb.** Debian's `chromium` package (bookworm security updates, a real stable channel build, arm64 native), pinned by version in the image and rebuilt when Debian publishes a security update. Playwright's own Chromium build for arm64 is the fallback if B1 finds Debian's unusable over CDP. Either way the browser is started by the service, not by Playwright.
- **Switches** (as `stage.py`, plus what a container needs): `--user-data-dir=/profiles/<profile id>`, `--remote-debugging-address=127.0.0.1 --remote-debugging-port=<per session>`, `--no-first-run --no-default-browser-check`, `--disable-blink-features=AutomationControlled` (with the debugging port only: nothing automates a person's own session), `--window-size=1440,900`, `--lang` from the languages of the `identity` section and `TZ` from `search.sweep_schedule.timezone` (it exists, default `Europe/Paris`; the identity section has a city and a country but no time zone, so a person with no sweep schedule gets the zone derived from the country), `--password-store=basic`, the preference `credentials_enable_service=false` and the managed policy `PasswordManagerEnabled: false` (the browser never saves a password), the managed policy `CommandLineFlagSecurityWarningsEnabled: false` (no warning about the switches across the top of the page the person sees), `--disable-features=Translate` (one switch, with `WebRtcHideLocalIpsWithMdns`, and `IsolateSandboxedIframes` in a sweep lease: B17), downloads into a per session directory nobody reads. Never `--no-sandbox` (below), never `--headless`.
- **Display:** one Xvfb screen of 1440 by 900 at 24 bits, the Chromium window maximised on it, one window per session. Fonts: Liberation, Noto (Latin, CJK subset), Noto Color Emoji, so pages render as on a desktop.

## Memory on the Jetson

- **Assumed figure: one live browser session costs about 950 MB at peak**: Chromium with one heavy application page 650 to 750 MB (browser, GPU, network and one or two renderer processes), the Playwright driver and `browserd` about 150 MB, Xvfb about 40 MB, x11vnc and websockify about 60 MB. **Container limit: 1,152 MB** (`mem_limit: 1152m`, `shm_size: 128m` counted inside it). With the 3,968 MB of `compose.yml` today, the stack totals exactly 5,120 MB, the 5G budget of SPEC-platform, instead of the 5,504 MB the reservation of 1,536 MB would cost.
- **Hence one Chromium at a time on the whole Jetson.** Sessions are leased one at a time; a second request waits in a queue (below). Two at once would need about 1.9 GB, which the Jetson does not have. Tab count per session is capped at 3; a page that pushes the container's working set (its memory less the inactive page cache, as `docker stats` counts it) past 90 % of its limit is closed by `browserd` (the tab, not the session), with the reason recorded.
- **B1 measures before anything else is built**: peak resident memory of the container for a blank page, an Ashby form, a Greenhouse form, a Workday form and LinkedIn's job search, each held 2 minutes with the live view connected. If the Workday peak passes 1,100 MB, B1 proposes the change (a lower parser limit, measured by ingestion, or Workday out of reach) and the owner sees it in the PR before B2 starts.

## The service

```
browser container (image jobapp-browser, arm64, Debian slim)
  tini -> browserd (Python, asyncio)            control API on :9300 (network browsing), shared secret
            Xvfb :1                            virtual screen
            x11vnc -> :5900 (localhost)        live view source, view only unless the person takes over
            websockify :6080 (network browsing), noVNC static files, token per lease
            chromium (one at a time)           CDP on 127.0.0.1 only, per lease
            Playwright (Python, connect_over_cdp to localhost)   the tool layer
```

- **`browserd`** is the only process anything outside the container talks to. Its control API (`BROWSER_CONTROL_SECRET` in `.env`, known to the api and the worker only): `POST /leases` (person id, profile id, purpose, automation on or off) answers a lease id and a lease token once Chromium is up; `DELETE /leases/{id}`; `GET /leases/current`; the **tool API** of the current lease (`POST /leases/{id}/tools/{name}`, lease token required), and `PUT /leases/{id}/files/{name}` to place a document for upload. CDP never leaves the container: the agents never get a CDP address.
- **Leases** are kept by the api in Redis (one key, compare and set), mirrored by `browserd`, which refuses a second lease. Purposes and their priority: `takeover` (the person in the live view) and the person's own sessions (`look`, `signin`, and `apply_manual` for Apply myself, apply A9) > `apply_fill` and `apply_submit` waited on by a person > `full_auto` > `sweep`. No preemption, with one exception: a `sweep` lease ends at once (after its current tool call) when a person asks for their own browser (`look` or `signin`), and the sweep resumes at the next sweep (B8); every other running lease ends first. Limits: 15 minutes of active time for an apply lease, 30 minutes for a person's session, 5 minutes without any input or tool call ends a lease. While a lease is `waiting_person` (the sign in hand off) neither the maximum nor the idle timer runs: the wait has its own deadline of 30 minutes, and the lease then resumes with the active time it had left, so a hand off in the middle of an application never ends the lease at 15 minutes. The maximum is a setting per purpose, because a Workday wizard at human pacing may need more than 15 minutes of active time: A15 measures it and proposes the figure; the queue page says "Your browser starts in about 6 minutes".
- **Chromium lifecycle:** started for a lease on the person's profile directory, closed at the end of the lease (profile flushed to disk). A crash or a hang (no CDP answer in 10 s) ends the lease with `browser_crashed`, a Chromium that quits by itself with status 0 (its window closed) with `browser_closed`; the next lease starts a fresh process. Every end, also one whose step fails, kills every process left under a profile uid, and every start does it again first, so nothing of an earlier lease is on the display. A session without a debugging port (a person's own session, the hand off) is watched through its process instead (B7): its exit ends the lease as above, a TCP port that a process of its uid listens on ends it `debugging_port`, the memory guard ends the whole session past its limit (no tab can be closed without CDP), and the end sends `SIGINT` to Chromium's browser process (SIGTERM exits without writing the last cookies, measured in B7), waits for the profile to be flushed and kills its process group after 10 s.

## How agents drive it

- **Decision taken: a narrow tool layer, not the raw Playwright MCP server.** The capability map names "Playwright MCP endpoint". The official `@playwright/mcp` server exposes `browser_evaluate` and `browser_run_code` (arbitrary JavaScript and Playwright code), uploads by any path and every tab, which no agent of this app may have. So `browserd` implements a short list of tools with the same names, arguments and accessibility snapshot format as Playwright MCP, and the worker exposes them to an agent as tools of its in-process MCP server (the agents runtime pattern of `api/app/agents/tools.py`: `register(name, factory)` and `create_sdk_mcp_server`; tool names must match `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,47}$`, which the `browser_*` names do), bound server side to the run's user and lease:
  `browser_snapshot` (the accessibility snapshot with element refs, cut at 40,000 characters, `native_files`: the file names in each native file input among its first 200 buttons, `[]` for a ref gone since the snapshot or past those 200, and `credentials`: whether each of its first 200 text entries is a credential input, a password box, an autocomplete of `current-password`, `new-password` or `one-time-code`, a `webauthn` box, or a `username` box of a form that holds one of those, read from the element and never from its label, a ref gone since the snapshot or past those 200 left out), `browser_navigate` (http and https only, to the hosts the lease allows), `browser_click`, `browser_type`, `browser_select_option`, `browser_check`, `browser_file_upload` (a document name the lease was given, never a path), `browser_press_key` (a short allow list), `browser_wait_for`, `browser_take_screenshot`, `browser_tabs` (list and select, no new tab to another host), and `browser_request_signin` (not in Playwright MCP: asks the person to sign in or sign up, see "The sign in hand off"; it takes a site name and a reason, never a credential).
- **What the tool layer enforces in code**, so no prompt can change it: human pacing (typing 60 to 140 ms per character with jitter, a scroll and a hover before every click, 1 to 3 s of dwell between actions, reading pauses on new pages); a host allow list per lease (the application's host, its ATS's asset and upload hosts, the identity providers the person signs in with); no `file:`, `chrome:`, `devtools:`, `view-source:` or `data:` navigation; refusal of a click on any element the submit detector of the apply module marks (submit, send, apply, postuler, candidater, envoyer, and the adapters' own selectors) unless the call comes from the apply gate's code path, which no agent can reach; refusal of buttons that delete, withdraw, pay or write to a recruiter ("Contacter le recruteur"); refusal of a typing into a credential input (`credential`, the same read as the snapshot's), whoever asks. A lease flagged `profile_edit` (SPEC-apply, "Complete my profile on a site") has its detector extended to Save, Update, Enregistrer and Mettre à jour, and only the profile gate's code path may click them. Page content is data, never instructions: every agent prompt that sees a snapshot says so.
- **Uploads:** the worker sends the frozen PDF bytes to `PUT /leases/{id}/files/{name}` (held in browserd's memory with the lease, at most 3 PDFs of 20 MB, gone with it; Chromium's uid never reads a file of them, the tool hands the bytes over) and the tool sets that file; file choosers (Google Careers' Browse button, or the one a person opens in apply's Apply myself, A9) are answered the same way, never by a dialog over a folder. B1 records whether `set_input_files` over a local CDP connection needs this; the design does not depend on it.
- **For development only,** the official Playwright MCP server or CLI can attach to a lease from inside the container (`docker compose exec`). It is never reachable from the network.

## The live view

- **noVNC** in the page, through Caddy at `/vnc/` (websocket to `browser:6080`). Caddy asks the api first (`forward_auth` to `GET /api/browser/live/auth`), which checks the session and a one use ticket bound to the person and the current lease (30 s to use, then a websocket that lives as long as the lease). A person who does not hold the current lease gets 403: they can never see another person's screen. The VNC password is per lease and never shown.
- **View only while an agent works.** "Take over" asks `browserd` to switch x11vnc to input mode (`x11vnc -R viewonly:0`) and pauses the run: the agent's next tool call waits until "Give back" or 15 minutes, then is told "The person used the browser; the page is now <title>". The person's keyboard and mouse never mix with the agent's. A takeover stops the lease's clock, and all the takeovers of one lease together stop it for 15 minutes at most: a later one ends the lease at what the earlier ones left, so the apply job's timeout, which allows one such pause, outlives them all. A person's own session (`look`, `signin`) has no agent: its lease is in the mode "the person holds the input" from its start to its end, with no Take over or Give back, and `browserd` refuses every tool call on it (B7).
- **Mobile:** the viewer scales to the screen and offers the noVNC on screen keyboard; a phone over the Tailscale Funnel is enough to solve a captcha. Expected traffic 100 to 500 KB/s while the page moves, near zero when still.
- **Rejected:** a stream of CDP screencast frames (no browser dialogs, no file chooser, no captcha frames from another origin in some cases, and a second input path to secure).

## Persistent profiles and logins

- **One profile directory per person, persisted** (`browser_profiles` table: `user_id`, a random `profile_id`, the Linux uid that owns it, size, last used). On the volume `browserprofiles`, mode 0700, each directory owned by its own uid (20000 plus a counter); `browserd` keeps only the capabilities to start Chromium under that uid and drops them in the child. **Every lease of the person starts on that same directory**, whatever its purpose (apply, sweep, hand off, complete my profile, "Look at my browser"), and ends by closing Chromium cleanly so the profile is flushed. Logins persist as cookies and local storage in that directory, like a desktop browser, so the next opening is connected. Cookies without an expiry date (session cookies) are kept across restarts with the setting "continue where you left off" (`session.restore_on_startup`); B7 proves it with a fixture that sets a session cookie. The tabs of the last session are not reopened: Chromium opened them beside the new session's address, a tab more at every lease, past the cap of 3 (issue #488), so `prep` removes them before every start and each session opens on its own address alone.
- **The sign in hand off** (the way a person signs in, whoever needs it). An agent that reaches a login or sign up wall, or an adapter that sees one, calls `browser_request_signin(site, reason)`. **When nobody watches** (a `sweep` or `full_auto` lease, or a lease no live view of the person is connected to), the tool answers `nobody_watching` at once and starts nothing: the work waits as `needs_person: signin` in its module (apply's "Needs you: sign in to <site>"); when the person opens it later, the hand off is a sign in session (below) at the form's address, and "I'm signed in" queues the work again (B12b). **When the person watches:** the lease keeps its place and waits for the person (`waiting_person: signin` in `browserd` and in the api in step, the idle timer and the maximum suspended, 30 minutes at most, a deadline that survives a Redis restart); the tool answers at once and the worker polls the lease's state, so a job that calls the tool outlives the wait (apply's fill job timeout adds `BROWSER_SESSION_MAX_S`) or ends its turn and is queued again; `browserd` notes the tab's address, closes Chromium and starts it again on the same profile, at that address, **without any debugging port** and with the live view in input mode, so Google, LinkedIn or an ATS sees an ordinary browser while the person types; the person is told the host of the address `browserd` noted ("Sign in to jobs.example.org, then click I'm signed in"), with a link that opens the live view on that tab, never the agent's `site` argument, which a hostile page can steer while it draws a fake sign in form; that host is where the hand off starts, and since the person's Chromium has no allow list, a page may still move him to another host afterwards (an open point of B12b); he signs in or signs up himself, with a password, "Sign in with Google" or an e-mail code, and clicks **I'm signed in**; `browserd` closes Chromium, starts it again with CDP on the same profile at the same address, and the worker's poll answers "The person signed in; the page is now <title>". The adapter then checks that the wall is gone, else it asks again. "Cancel" or 30 minutes without a click ends the hand off: the lease is released and the work waits as `needs_person` in the apply module's queue. The app never sees what is typed: keystrokes go from the viewer to the browser, nothing is logged.
- **"Sign in to a site"** in Account is the same mechanism started by the person, without an agent: a session with no debugging port, at an address he types or picks (http or https with a host only, passed to Chromium after `--` so it is never read as a switch). "Look at my browser" runs without a debugging port too (B7). Every chrome:// page (chrome://flags, chrome://settings, chrome://extensions, chrome://inspect, ...) is blocked by the image's managed policy in every session, the agents' leases and the person's own (owner, 2026-10-20), but chrome://policy and chrome://sandbox (the test probes), the new tab page and the browser's own UI (chrome://*.top-chrome); chrome://policy's test page is off (`PolicyTestPageEnabled` false: its "Apply policies" replaced the image's policy and reopened chrome://flags), and no extension may be installed. Before every start prep turns chrome://inspect's remote debugging setting off (once on, it opens a DevTools port at every later start, measured in B7) and drops from Local State the chrome://flags entries, which keep applying once the page is blocked, and the policy set chrome://policy/test's "Restart and apply" saves for the next start.
- **A job website signed in once (B17, #714):** each website of Find jobs has a Sign in action, the same session as "Sign in to a site" at that website's address; Done ends it and records the website as signed in for that person's profile only when browserd finds an unexpired cookie of its host in the profile (host and expiry read, never a value). Searches then read that website in the person's own persisted profile with JavaScript on, under the request rule of a signed in search (GET and HEAD only, but a page's fetch or XHR POST to its platform's hosts or search service (B19), never to an address whose path applies, submits, deletes or pays, nor one whose path or query applies, submits or signs the person out, the person's own search words aside, the path read percent decoded; service workers bypassed and out of the page's reach; no WebSocket or worker; the same in every frame of the page, an inline sandboxed one included, as a sweep lease runs Chromium with `IsolateSandboxedIframes` disabled); every other website stays anonymous with JavaScript off. A sign in page met while signed in ends the website `platform_signin` and asks the person to sign in again. Forget all logins removes every website's login.
- **What is never stored:** passwords (the browser's password manager is off; the app has no password vault), payment cards (autofill of cards off). The app never chooses a password and never creates an account by itself.
- **Cookies on Linux are protected by a fixed key** when no keyring exists, so the volume is as sensitive as the logins. **Encryption at rest, stated plainly:** the app adds none. The Jetson's disk is not encrypted (SPEC-platform plans no disk encryption), so the profile files are readable in the clear by `root` on the Jetson, that is, by the administrator who holds the shell (today the owner), and by anyone who has the physical disk. Inside the stack only `browserd` and the Chromium of that person's own uid (mode 0700) can read a profile; no other service mounts the volume, and no route of the api returns a profile file. It is excluded from the nightly backup (losing it costs the person their logins, nothing else) and from any dump, and it is deleted with the account (the cascade queues the directory's removal, B3; B7 drains that queue through `browserd` and writes the profile's size at every lease end). If the owner wants the disk encrypted, that is a platform decision, not this module's. "Forget all logins" deletes the directory; the next lease starts empty and the sites ask again. A profile over 1 GB is trimmed of its caches at lease end.

## Isolation and network

- **Between people:** one lease at a time, one profile directory per uid, a lease token per lease, the live view ticket per person and lease, uploads per lease in browserd's memory, gone with the lease. The worker passes the run's user id to `browserd`, never a profile path. Tests in every task prove that person B gets 404 or 403 on A's lease, view, files and profile.
- **Chromium's own sandbox stays on.** The container gets the seccomp profile that permits user namespaces for Chromium (no `SYS_ADMIN`, no `--privileged`), `no-new-privileges`, a read only root, `cap_drop: ALL` plus `SETUID` and `SETGID` for `browserd`. B1 checks that the Jetson kernel allows unprivileged user namespaces; if not, the setuid sandbox helper is the fallback, never `--no-sandbox`.
- **Networks:** `browser` is on a new network `egress` (the only one with a route out, used by no other service) and on `browsing` (internal: `browserd`'s control port and websockify, reached by the api, the worker and Caddy). It is not on `default`, so it cannot resolve db, redis or the api. A host firewall rule (`DOCKER-USER`, installed by `deploy.sh`) drops traffic from the `egress` subnet to private, link local and carrier grade NAT ranges (the home network, the router, the Jetson itself, the tailnet's 100.64.0.0/10), so a hostile page can reach the internet and nothing else.

## Bot detection: what remains and what reduces it

- **The address is good:** the Jetson goes out through the owner's home connection, a residential address, not a datacenter. What remains: every person of the app shares that one address, so the app paces per host across people (apply: one submission per host every 3 minutes, sweeps one page every 5 to 15 s).
- **The fingerprint is not a desktop's:** `X11; Linux x86_64` in the user agent with the client hint architecture `arm` (what Debian's arm64 Chromium reports, B1; never faked, a fingerprint that lies is not allowed), the brand "Chromium" in client hints instead of "Google Chrome", WebGL through a software renderer (no GPU in Xvfb). These are honest values; faking them is refused (a mismatched fingerprint is easier to spot than a rare one). Reduced by: a headed browser with a real window, a persistent profile that ages like a person's, the person's language and time zone, cookies of sites the person signs in to (a signed in Google account raises reCAPTCHA scores), no automation switches, no `navigator.webdriver`, the human pacing of the tool layer.
- **What the design does when detection wins:** a captcha or a block page ends automatic work on that page and asks the person, in the live view; Full auto sends the application to the review queue. The app never uses a captcha solving service.

## Interactive discovery (#626)

The owner requested actual model-driven Playwright search on chosen websites and criteria on 2026-10-24. The owner-facing contract and acceptance evidence live in [SPEC-jobs](SPEC-jobs.md#interactive-search-626). These approved browser boundaries extend discovery beyond the old finder; they do not declare support shipped or activate a platform.

- Give the configured runtime the frozen source/criteria scope and only the existing user/lease-bound navigate, snapshot, type, click, select/check and wait operations needed for search, pagination and posting details, plus the guarded sign in handoff. The model chooses actions from observed pages. Page text and custom instructions cannot grant tools or permissions.
- Search-query and filter submission need a bounded discovery permission proved by tool policy. They confer no application or profile gate capability: clicks, Enter and typed submission that apply, send, contact, save a profile or change an account remain refused, including misleading controls. Keep the application-submit detector and approval gate protections; no broad exemption for forms labelled Search.
- Keep the selected hosts and approved posting destinations enforced by code, including redirects. A job website's start page that redirects on its own host (a locale path) is followed under the rules of a navigation (jobs J18). A platform's hosts are a family (owner, 2026-10-29, B19, [decisions](docs/decisions.md)): Indeed `indeed.com`, `www.indeed.com` and every `<two letters>.indeed.com` (the country sites of 2026-10-28), LinkedIn the same under `linkedin.com`, Welcome to the Jungle `welcometothejungle.com` and its `www.` host, https only; the lease opens the family, browserd's allow list and the api's snapshot check hold every page to it, and a redirect within it is followed; one to the platform's sign in host (`secure.indeed.com`) or a sign in path is the sign in wall (`platform_signin`, the login marked expired); a redirect anywhere else stays refused, and browserd's warning names the stopped target's host, never its path or query. No raw CDP, arbitrary evaluate/JavaScript, filesystem access, automatic CAPTCHA solving or automation during person-held input. A login/checkpoint uses the existing live view, takeover and manual handoff with the same person's persistent profile; never ask the model for credentials.

Ticking a platform in Find jobs and saving records its consent through that platform's consent route (owner, 2026-10-27, jobs J17); the saved selection alone never satisfies it, and nothing clears a pause. The platform consent foundation of #447 or draft #619 alone does not deliver this search contract.
- One agent per job website (owner, 2026-10-27, jobs J18): "An LLM agent for each website that will search intelligently, just like a human, using all the parameters provided to it." A website is one lease and one finder run a sweep, given all the person's criteria as context (wanted and unwanted titles and places, contract types, remote stance, seniority, industries, company types, pay, recency and the custom instructions, never typed); it decides which searches to run, in which order, and when the website is covered. Every criterion goes into its prompt and it reads each as the person meant it (owner, 2026-10-28: "all the parameters should be injected into the prompt of the agent who does the search"): a place such as "everywhere", "anywhere", "worldwide" or "partout" means no place, so it leaves the place box empty. What it may type stays the person's own words: in a search box one saved wanted title, in the place box one saved wanted place (a regular expression only when it is a plain word), each as saved or with another case or accents ("GenAI Engineer" for "genai engineer", "Ile-de-France" for "Île-de-France"), or nothing: leaving a box empty is always allowed. A translation of a saved word is refused, since code cannot check it. A native filter takes only the saved remote stance or the one saved contract type (several are checked after reading), case and accents aside. browserd's frozen scope carries those sets of values and refuses any other, and a search request carries a saved title, a saved place or none, and every saved filter. The api tells the model its own slips first: a typed value its box does not take (a saved place in a title box, a value never saved; the box told from its role and accessible name, `finder.box_of`, any saved value going on to browserd where it cannot tell) or a choice that is no saved value is refused before the browser as a tool error the model can correct, counted apart (`refused.model_value`), and is no reason to end the website, and a model that only slips stops with its share of the sweep's time. How a website ends is what its agent read and said, never which tools it used (2026-10-28, issue 716, `interactive.outcome`): it was searched when the agent reported a verified posting, or said after a search the session saw that the website listed no matching job (`report_no_postings` no_match); a search the session saw is a page whose address carries a saved title, or a button click or an Enter in the box (browser_type's `submit`, B19) after which the page shows the title typed in a text entry or its address, never a cookie banner's decline (browserd marks it `declined`) nor a link to an address without a saved title; else the blocker it named ends it with its own code (`no_job_list`, `cookie_wall`, `signin_wall`, `no_search_box`); else a refusal of browserd's guards or of the website ends it `unsupported_interaction`; else `nothing_reported`, never a success. A click the page did not let finish (browserd's `page_error`, `element_covered` when another element such as a cookie banner covers it, `stale_ref`) is told to the agent to recover from, until the website's share of the sweep's time is spent; browserd's `guard_failed` (a tab off the list it could not close, a page it could not take back) ends the website with that code. browserd takes one click besides a native search or a link: a cookie banner's decline button (`discovery.decline`), never accepting; the agent may open the website's search page by its address with no query, at `/<endpoint>` or `/<language>/<endpoint>` only (`/jobs`, `/fr/jobs`, never `/settings/jobs`; a search by address only from a link a page showed), and browserd opens that page as any page, while a search request still carries a saved title. The scope carries at most 100 saved titles, 100 saved places and 32 KiB as sent: a person who saved more has the first ones that fit searched, in saved order (a title then a place in turn), and the summary says how many were left out. Its budget per website is "no limit" (owner): no cap on its model turns, pages, tokens or verified postings. What bounds it is not its budget: the sweep's outer limit of 900 seconds, shared fairly among the websites still to run (a website gets the sweep's time left divided by the websites left, and is told the seconds it has left); the lease's own maximum; pacing per platform and the 7 day pause (no website has a daily limit or a page cap, LinkedIn included: owner, 2026-10-28); and the agents runtime's own ceilings, raised for this agent so they never cut it first (max_turns 10,000, wall time 900 seconds). Existing lease and handoff deadlines remain; report which limit stopped work and list selected sources left unattempted. Any changed cap needs evidence and a reviewed spec change.

| Method and path | Does |
|---|---|
| GET /api/browser | the person's browser: profile exists, size, last used, current lease (theirs only), queue position |

| POST /api/browser/handoff/later ; POST /api/browser/handoff/later/done ; POST /api/browser/handoff/later/cancel `{module, ref}` | the later hand off of work that waits as `needs_person: signin` (its module registers it, apply's `application`): a sign in session at the address the module stores for the person's own item (404 for none, 409 while the browser holds other work, 422 for an address no longer valid, 503 without Redis or browserd); "I'm signed in" ends it and queues the work again once; "Cancel" ends it and the work keeps waiting |
| GET and PUT /api/browser/linkedin | the LinkedIn switch of the person: off by default; turning it on records the acceptance of the sentence (date and version) |

## Frontend

Account: "Your browser" card (status, Sign in to a site, a slot for "Complete my profile on a site" that the apply module fills, the LinkedIn switch with its sentence, Forget all logins with a confirmation that names what is lost). A hand off banner wherever a run waits for a sign in (the job page and the queue; the app shell's dot is apply A10a's): the site, "Open the live view", "I'm signed in", "Cancel". A live view component (`<LiveBrowser lease=... />`) used by Account, by the job page of the apply module and by the page of a website search run (B18, below): the screen, Take over and Give back, full screen, a status line ("The assistant is filling the form", "Waiting for you: a captcha"). The queue position while waiting. Mobile friendly.

**The page of a website search run (B18, owner request of 2026-10-29).** `/runs/:id` of a `browser_discovery` run shows the live view while the run runs, on the person's current lease when it is the sweep's (`holder` "you", `automation`, purpose `sweep`, not `starting`, started at or before the run): watching by default, with the LiveBrowser's own Take over and Give back, which pause the agent's next tool call and end neither the lease nor the search by themselves (the website's share of the sweep's time and the lease's budget go on during a Take over, so a long one can end this website's search). While the run is queued, or has no lease yet, or its lease starts, the page says "Your browser is starting."; once the run ended, or the lease is another one, one line says "The browser shows here while this website is searched." No recording, no screenshot, no other agent's page, and nothing the view adds starts, ends or takes a lease (the run's own Cancel stops this website's search, which then gives its lease back).

## Testing strategy

- Unit: lease state machine and priorities, ticket signing and expiry, host allow list, the submit and danger detectors, pacing bounds, profile uid allocation.
- Integration: the real browser container in a test stack (`tests/browser.compose.yml`) on the Mac (arm64 Docker, the same image as the Jetson) against the fake ATS pages of the apply module; isolation between two people; network tests from inside the container (db, redis, the router's address and the host refused; a public test page reached through a stub).
- Journeys (Playwright on the Mac driving the app's page, which shows the live view of the container's Chromium).
- On the Jetson: memory and start time measured (B1, Checkpoint B).

## Boundaries

- **Always:** one session at a time; the person can watch and take over; the sandbox on; pacing in code.
- **Ask first:** a second concurrent browser; any captcha service; LinkedIn beyond the owner's decision; giving an agent a tool outside the list above.
- **Never:** CDP outside the container; `--no-sandbox` or `--headless`; storing a password, choosing one or creating an account for a person; one person's profile, screen or files reachable by another; a fingerprint that lies.

## Success criteria

1. On the Jetson, the owner opens "Your browser" from his phone, signs in to a site, and is still signed in the next day.
2. An agent fills a fake form in the browser while he watches, he takes over, gives back, and the agent continues.
3. The stack stays within 5G with the browser running a heavy page (measured).
4. No person can reach another's browser, and the browser cannot reach the home network or the stack's databases (tests).
5. A run that meets a login wall waits; the owner signs in himself in the live view, clicks "I'm signed in", the run goes on, and the next day's run is already connected to that site.
