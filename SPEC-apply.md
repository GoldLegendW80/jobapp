# Spec: apply

Module of [CAPABILITY-MAP.md](CAPABILITY-MAP.md). Depends on `agents`, `documents`, `browser`, `jobs`, `profile`, and on `mail` for applications by e-mail. Umbrella issue: #69; the review queue after a Like is #78, Full auto is #79. Plan: [tasks/apply/todo.md](tasks/apply/todo.md). Intent: `docs/intent/swipe-onboarding-opportunities.md` of the job workspace (decisions of 2026-10-10). The owner's decisions of 2026-10-14 are taken below. Implementation and verification progress is tracked in [the completion checklist](tasks/apply/completion.md).

## Objective

Once a posting has its CV and letter (documents module), the app applies: it opens the application form in the person's browser on the Jetson, uploads the documents, answers the questions from the person's profile, and submits, then records the application, moves the posting to `applied` and keeps a report with screenshots of the form before and after. The person chooses how much they want to see: they apply themselves with the agent's help, they approve each filled form, or, in Full auto, a Like is enough.

User stories:
- As a person, I click **Apply** on a posting, watch the agent fill the form live, check the answers and the screenshot, click **Approve and submit**, and the posting moves to `applied`.
- As a person, I click **Apply myself** and the form opens in my browser with my documents ready to upload; I submit it and the app records it.
- As a person who Likes postings, I find each filled application in the **review queue**, edit an answer, approve or reject it (#78).
- As a person in **Full auto**, a Like leads to a submitted application within limits I set, and I can cancel it during the hold, pause everything with one switch, and read what was sent (#79).
- As a person, when a site needs an account, I sign in or sign up myself in the live view, and the application goes on where it stopped; the next time, I am already signed in.
- As a person, I click **Complete my profile on this site** (Workday, LinkedIn, a company portal), review each section the agent filled from my profile, and approve it before it is saved.
- As a person, I never see an answer the agent made up, a box ticked that commits me to more than the application, or two applications to the same role.

## What the command line teaches (`engine/stage.py`, `engine/vie.py`, `AGENTS.md` "Applying" in the parent folder)

- `stage.py` opens the form in a real Chrome over CDP, rewrites the posting address to the form (Ashby `/application`, Lever `/apply`, Greenhouse's embed page, a VIE offer's external Apply link), uploads the CV to the first resume input and the letter to a cover letter input (or pastes it in the additional information box), then answers yes or no groups, radios, checkboxes, native selects, text inputs and react-select comboboxes from a table of patterns, and prints what it **left** (EEO, salary, anything it cannot map). It never clicks Submit unless told to, and its `submit` reads the page afterwards for a confirmation or the form's errors.
- Lessons kept: headed, persistent profile, human pacing and dwell pass Ashby's invisible reCAPTCHA where headless fails; Ashby yes or no groups are `button[data-option=yes|no]`; Greenhouse selects are react-select comboboxes (type, then click the option); Google Careers' résumé goes through a file chooser and its element ids change on every render (locate by label or role); ElevenLabs' location is a country list; a VIE offer applies on the site's own panel only for a signed in candidate with a complete profile.
- What changes: the table of answers was the owner's own (his name, his dates, his choices); here it is built from each person's profile. `stage.py` ticked arbitration and terms boxes by pattern; here such boxes follow "What the agent may never do". The harness that ran the command line refused to automate the final click; here the click is made by the app's code, never by a model, under the rules of "Who clicks Submit".

## Owner decisions (taken on 2026-10-14)

1. **Full auto: guarded defaults.** The owner's words: "Guarded defaults", as the draft recommended. Full auto is **off until the person turns it on** (Review first is the default, intent of 2026-10-10). Then: only postings at or above the person's fit threshold (decision 2); at most **one application per company in 30 days** (unless a company cap of the person says otherwise); a **hold of at least 15 minutes** between the decision and the submission, during which a Cancel stops it; **Ashby, Greenhouse and Lever forms first** (e-mail applications once A14 lands and LinkedIn Easy Apply once A16 lands, decision 4), everything else goes to the review queue; and the **first 3 applications in Full auto are rehearsals** (filled and screenshotted, not submitted, confirmed by the person) before the mode goes live.
2. **Daily limit: by fit score only.** The owner's words: "By fit score only". The fit threshold decides which postings Full auto applies to. It is the same family of setting as the documents module's automatic generation (merged: `search.documents: {auto, min_fit, daily_ceiling}`): the same range, 0 to 100. Three thresholds then exist and each card names its own, so nobody confuses them: "Show postings from" (`search.min_fit`, which exists on main with the default 70: the sweep's threshold), "Write documents from" (`search.documents.min_fit`) and "Apply automatically from" (`apply_settings.min_fit`, this module's). When the person turns Full auto on he confirms the last one, prefilled with `search.documents.min_fit`, else 80 (a builders' default, shown and editable). There is **no fixed daily number of applications**. A **safety ceiling of 30 submissions a day** (`safety_ceiling`, in Advanced settings, counted in the time zone of `search.sweep_schedule.timezone`) is a runaway backstop only: when it is reached, the rest of the day's items go to the review queue with the reason `safety_ceiling`, and nothing is lost. The old fixed 10 a day is removed.
3. **Accounts on job sites.** The owner's words: "The agent open playwright, user sign in or log in, we save all the playwright profile per user so next opening will be connected. User can ask to complete his profile on the website with all informations." So: the app never chooses a password and never creates an account; when a site needs one, the agent opens the page in the person's browser session on the Jetson and the person signs in or signs up himself in the live view (the sign in hand off of SPEC-browser); the per person browser profile is persisted, so the next run is already connected; and a new action, **Complete my profile on this site**, lets the person ask the agent to fill the site's candidate profile (Workday, LinkedIn, a company portal) from the app's profile, with the guardrails of a form fill (no invented answers, screenshots, a review before saving where the site allows).
4. **LinkedIn: full use.** The owner's words: "Full use". Searches for jobs and Easy Apply, in Full auto too, from the person's own signed in session. Human pacing, a low request rate and the per company and per day guards stay (LinkedIn's own pace: one Easy Apply every 10 minutes, 15 a day at most). It is a per person switch, off by default; turning it on shows one sentence that the person accepts once: LinkedIn's terms forbid automated use of its site and the account may be limited or closed (the text is in SPEC-browser). Without that acceptance, no LinkedIn work starts.

## The flow of one application

1. **Start** (the Apply button, a Like, or an approval in the queue; a Like goes through `apply.on_like(db, user_id, posting_id)`, the contract with the swipe deck of product #74, and is the application itself: created at once with the mode in force, it waits for its documents): refusals before anything is spent (below); an `applications` row; a browser lease is queued (since 2026-11-06 a fill, on a lease naming its application, takes a free slot of the person's browser beside a search or another fill, and waits in line when none is free; the apply queue runs BROWSER_SLOTS jobs and the tick asks for up to that many of a person's applications; the submit stays watch only: [SPEC-browser, Two browsers at once](SPEC-browser.md#two-browsers-at-once-owner-2026-11-06)).
2. **Fill:** the lease opens the form address (the ATS strategy rewrites the posting address as `stage.py` does); the documents' versions to send are fixed through `documents.current` (documents D14: the approved version of each, else the latest rendered one, with its fit and its corrections; a posting whose documents are still being written waits with their reason). Apply on a posting without its CV or letter (owner, product audit chapter 2, answer 5 of 2026-11-05: "Apply with no documents writes them and then sends") asks them as a Like does (`likes.on_apply`: the same writers, fact check, consent and credential waits; a click, never held by the threshold or the daily ceiling) and keeps the kinds asked (`documents_asked`): the application waits for them, then is filled and sent, or goes to review in Review first. An asked document that ends without a version (its writer failed, the person stopped it, or a letter that never began 10 minutes after its CV) ends it `blocked` with `documents_not_written`, nothing sent; without any CV and none coming the code stays `no_documents`. Apply is also on each row of the Jobs list, under the job page's conditions, behind the same confirmation as a selection of one (owner, 2026-11-06: "yes confirmation"), and in the bulk bar of the Jobs list and of the map for the selected postings (owner, 2026-11-06: "in the job list and map, i want being able with the multi select to apply at all selected"; API below), each started as by its own Apply click. The documents module corrects itself and never blocks (its decision), so nothing here is refused on content: the fit and the corrections are stored on the application and shown, and Full auto's rule below uses them; the strategy's adapter fills what it knows deterministically; the answer resolver fills the rest from the profile; the agent handles only what neither recognises; everything else is **left**. The posting moves to `staged` (the app filled its form: the mail module's binding rule relies on this).
While an approved CV or letter version is still queued or rendering, the run waits for that version (`documents:rendering`) instead of filling an older one. The gate refuses an approval meanwhile (`documents_changed`). A render still queued or running an hour after its wait began ends the application `failed` with `documents_rendering`, nothing sent. Polling, another waiting reason or restarting the same browser lease does not refresh that hour; a genuinely new fill attempt starts a new bound. Apply myself follows the same approved-version wait and one-hour bound. A renderer that reports a failure is no longer pending: the documents module then chooses the latest rendered version by its existing rule.

3. **Screenshot "before"** (full page), and the list of fields with each value and its source.
4. **Decision** by the gate; in Full auto the item is then `held` for at least 15 minutes (Cancel stops it), and the submit lease checks again just before the click (the kill switches, the pause, the caps and the pace, Full auto still on, the Like still valid, the read back); then **submit**, then **screenshot "after"** and the outcome.
5. **Record:** `applied` through the jobs module's `set_status` (event `via: "apply"`, `applied_on` filled), the document versions that were sent frozen by `documents.freeze(db, user_id, version_id, FreezeReason.apply, at)` (documents D14), in the same transaction that records the submission, the run report. The freeze happens only here, after the click, never at the decision; it never refuses. Documents' own hook on `applied` then finds them already frozen.

One click Apply (`direct`, A19, owner 2026-11-05; `applier.py`): step 1 as above (`service.start`: never apply, duplicates, an e-mail posting made Review first); then one run of the apply agent, which the fill job (`worker.apply_fill`) starts in place of steps 2 to 4. Before the lease: never apply again and LinkedIn's sentence (`blocked`), the emergency stop (`blocked` with `submit_disabled` or `paused`), the documents through `documents_of` as step 2 reads them. The agent's first message holds the posting (title, company, address, location, text), the person's profile sections as the documents writers and the answer resolver read them (contact details included), the Saved answers, the person's answers to its earlier questions, the letter's text and the names of the PDFs placed on the lease (`cv.pdf`, `letter.pdf`). It opens the posting, finds the application, fills, writes its own free text, uploads, moves through every step and clicks Submit itself. The emergency stop is read before every act of the agent (a control browserd does not mark may send too: on, the capability is withdrawn and the run ends with its code), and again before each of its acts on a submit element (browserd answers `submit_permit` without the lease's capability): on, the run ends with its code and nothing is sent; off, the application is `submitting` (committed before that first act) and the act is sent with the capability. The person's Cancel is read before every tool call of the agent, which stops at its next one. On this lease a send is also marked by German and French words ("Bewerbung absenden", "Abschicken", "Einreichen", "Envoyer ma candidature") and by a wizard's last word alone ("Finish", "Done", "Terminer", "Fertig") on a button. The end: `submitted` when the agent reports the confirmation it read since its last submit act (`apply_done`, its summary and the page's address in the run report's `agent:report` row), `applied` through `set_status` and the sent versions frozen in that transaction (`submit.record`); `submit_unknown` when it ends after a submit act without one, or crashes, or is cancelled (never sent again); `needs_person` (`agent_question`) when, before any submit act, it asked the person with nobody watching: the job page shows a fixed sentence with the field's label and the page's host, a text box for a fact (its answer goes to the next run, and to Saved answers with "Remember this answer") or the site's own session for an action, and the answer queues the application again; the label must be one the last snapshot shows, and a label that reads as a password, a code or a payment is always an action, never a text box. A question nobody watches after a submit act (a cookie banner's Accept or a wizard's Next is a submit in HTML, and the app cannot tell it from the last one) ends `submit_unknown`, never sent again by the app itself: the job page shows the question and "The assistant may have sent this application already, before it stopped here. Look for a confirmation mail or on the site first.", and the person's answer ("Try again with this answer") is their Try again, as Try again itself is for an application the agent may have sent: `submit_unknown` to `queued` (for `direct`, by the person only, a `retry` event), one more run, whose first message says an earlier run may have sent it (`earlier_send`), so the agent looks on the site (an "already applied" notice, a confirmation, the account's applications) and reports `submitted` with that page instead of sending again. `busy` (the person's agent runs all taken) queues it again after the retry delays; `failed` otherwise, with no retry. Applications that were already filled under the 2026-11-01 flow when A19 shipped are decided by it (`direct.decide`). A rehearsal (`dry_run`) never runs the apply agent.

No question to the person (A20, owner 2026-11-06: "I just want that the agent do all the work to apply and he never ask me a question"): since A20 the apply agent has no `browser_ask_person` in either box state and never creates a question. A required field the profile does not answer gets the answer a careful applicant would give for this person, consistent with the profile (a neutral option such as "Prefer not to say", "Other" or "Not applicable" for a personal fact; free text written from the profile), and each such answer is in the run report as chosen by the assistant (`chosen`: the field's label, the value, one line why), shown on the job page under "Answers the assistant chose"; a chosen entry is kept only for a field the run filled. A sign in the browser profile does not hold: the agent first looks for a way to apply without an account (guest apply, "continue without an account", "apply with your resume"); that, a password or one time code box, a code sent by mail or SMS, a captcha it cannot pass or a payment ends the application `failed` with one plain sentence of what the site asks and what the person can do (its `summary`, shown on the job page). The CV and the letter go to every resume widget (a file input, a button or a drop zone that opens the file chooser, a "Choose one" list of files with an upload option, a step that asks for the resume's text). The questions above (`needs_person` `agent_question`, `POST /api/applications/{id}/question`) remain only for an application that waits with an A19 question asked before A20: its answer still queues it again, and the next run gets it.

**Review first with the apply agent** (A20, owner 2026-11-06: "when I'm not in full auto mode, I want to see all the input he filled in but don't matter of the website or whatever, then a button to confirm"; `apply/confirm.py`). The Apply button with the review box ticked starts a Review first application marked `agent_review` at the click (not a Like, not a rehearsal, not an e-mail application: those keep the 2026-11-01 build). The fill job runs the apply agent of One click Apply, its first message with `review_first`, on a lease of purpose `apply_review`: browserd judges its acts as on `apply_agent`, but refuses (`submit_permit`) each act on a submit element without the capability, a button whose every name is a wizard's step phrase ("Next", "Continue", "Save and continue", "Review", "Suivant", "Weiter", and the others of `detect.STEP_PHRASES`) and a `<button>` without a type (a form's default submit: an upload picker, "Add another", Yes or No) whose names hold no word of a send aside, each such pass traced by browserd (`step_submit`, the event `agent_step_submit`) as a possible send, and the agent's session never holds the capability; with it, only a `browser_click` passes on this lease. No adapter, answer resolver, form agent, field left for the person or question. On the last step the agent calls `apply_ready(submit_ref, chosen)`, naming the control that sends the form by a ref of its last snapshot; the application is then `ready` with the list of every field the agent filled, in the order it filled them, by step (the page's first heading, else its title), each with the label the snapshot gave the control and the value it typed, chose, ticked or uploaded (a file by its name), a choice made by a click too (a radio, a checkbox, a switch, an option of a custom combobox or listbox, a button of a group: by the group's name, or the list the agent opened), keyed by the control so two fields of one label stay two, the answers it chose marked, and an answer it says it chose that no recorded act matches listed under "Answers the assistant chose"; the list comes from the acts the tools recorded, never a site specific reader. The fill job hands the held session to a job of its own queue (`jobapp:apply_hold`, at most 2 at once, one per person), so the wait never takes the apply queue's slot; that job holds the lease while it waits (a call every 60 s keeps the idle timer away and writes a beat), at most 15 minutes, and gives the browser up (`session_lost`) at its next poll when another person's lease waits, or a submit or an own session of the same person does; the person's own other fills wait behind it. The job page shows the list (the shared table) with **Confirm and send** and Cancel. Confirm (`POST /api/applications/{id}/confirm` with the list's hash) is offered and accepted only while a held session of the current run waits (its `agent_ready` newer than the last Try again and the last end, a beat within 180 s): it is refused `stale` on another list and `session_lost` otherwise (the application is then `failed`, nothing sent, and Try again is a new fill and a new list); else it writes a `person_approval` authorisation bound to the list's hash and moves `ready` to `approved`. The held job then reads the emergency stop (on: `blocked`), reads the page again and refuses `form_changed` (`failed`, nothing sent) when it differs from the page the list was read from (refs aside) or the named control is not found once by its role and name, uses the authorisation and commits `submitting`, and the gate clicks the control with the lease's capability; the agent then reads the page only and reports, recorded as One click Apply's recording does: `submitted` on a confirmation read since the click, else `submit_unknown`, never sent again. Cancel ends the wait, nothing sent; the wait's bound, a lost lease, a crash or a worker's start end it `failed` (`session_lost`), nothing sent. A confirmation the agent reports before any click of the gate (a step control that sent the form), and any end but `ready` after a traced step act (the agent failed, a crash, a lost lease, a lost hold), is `submit_unknown` with `sent_before_confirm`, never sent again by the app. Try again of such an application, or of a `submit_unknown` after the gate's click, is one more run of the agent in review mode that looks on the site first (`earlier_send`), never the old list. In Review first `browser_evaluate` is not available to the agent. The old Approve and submit is refused for such an application. The review queue shows such an item with a link to the job page ("Check what the assistant filled and confirm") in place of Approve.

Risks of One click Apply, accepted with the owner's "no guardrails" (2026-11-05): the agent submits applications by itself in the person's signed in accounts, for every person of the app, friends included, so a wrong or invented answer, the wrong form, an application to a posting the person did not mean or a legal box ticked cannot be taken back; there is no pace per host across applications, so LinkedIn and the ATSs can flag the account or the Jetson's address; the full profile, contact details included, is in the model's context while it reads untrusted pages (B20's privacy risk, larger here); there are no screenshots, so what was sent is the agent's own account of it (the run report).

In Review first, step 2 runs when the person clicks Apply (they watch) or after a Like (queued). The approval may come hours later: the submit lease re-opens the form, refills it from the **approved answers only** (no model call), checks that each value reads back equal to the approved one, takes the "before" screenshot again, and submits. A difference refuses the submit (`form_changed`) and returns the item to the queue.

## States

`applications.state`: `queued` → `filling` → `needs_person` (captcha, sign in, an account, a required field left) or `ready` (filled, waiting for approval) → `approved` or `held` (Full auto's hold) → `submitting` → `submitted`, or `submit_unknown`, or `refused_by_site` (the form showed errors after the click). Also `rehearsed`, `cancelled`, `blocked` (a guardrail refused it, with its code), `posting_closed`. Only the gate's decision enters `approved` or `held`, each time with an unused authorisation, so every way back to `ready` needs a new approval before another click. `submitting` is committed just before the click and ends only in the three outcomes of a click: a crash, a cancelled job or a dead worker after it is `submit_unknown`, never `failed` or `cancelled`. A refusal proven to come before the click (browserd refused the input, or the gate's checks again between the arm and the click) moves it back to `ready`, or to `blocked` or `posting_closed` as the same refusal before the arm would, the approval spent and no version frozen. Before the click the submit lease may send `approved` back to `ready` (`form_changed`, the approval spent), to `needs_person` (a captcha on the refilled form) or to `cancelled` (a kill switch). A rehearsal (`dry_run`) never reaches `submitting`: it is `rehearsed` from `approved` or `held` after the refill and the read back (apply A6). `rehearsed` stays an end that holds neither the posting nor the form address, with one exit: a Full auto rehearsal the person answers Yes to moves from `rehearsed` to `ready` in the review queue, as a `review` item without `dry_run`, in one transaction under the posting's row lock, refused 409 when another live application took the posting or its form address since the rehearsal (A13a). A manual application (Apply myself) moves to `submitted` without `submitting` when the person records it, or when its session ends (the lease, the job or the worker) after the page showed the site's confirmation, the one such move, for `manual` only (A9: the versions placed on the lease are frozen in that transaction); a session that ends before a confirmation sends it back to `queued` with `needs` `session_ended`, still holding the posting, so the person can open it again or say "I submitted it". A `submit_unknown` application is **never submitted again by the app itself**: a second application is worse than a missed one. The person sees "We could not confirm this application. Look for a confirmation mail or on the site." with "It was submitted" and "Try again" (a new approval: "Try again" on a Full auto item makes it a review item, so only the person's Approve clicks again, never the same Like; A13a). A confirmation mail from the ATS still moves the posting to `applied` through the mail module.

## Per ATS strategies

## Accounts and signing in (owner decision 3)

- The app never creates an account, never chooses a password and never types one. When a form needs an account (a Workday tenant, a company portal, Google Careers, the VIE site, LinkedIn), the adapter or the agent calls the sign in hand off of SPEC-browser: the run pauses with `needs_person: signin`, the person signs in or signs up himself in the live view (a password, "Sign in with Google", an e-mail code), clicks "I'm signed in", and the run continues at the same page. When nobody watches (every Full auto item, and a Review first item no live view of the person watches when the wall comes, such as the fill after a Like), the hand off answers `nobody_watching` at once: the item goes to the review queue as "Needs you: sign in to <site>"; when the person opens it, a sign in session of SPEC-browser opens at the form's address, and "I'm signed in" queues the fill again (A10b). Until the hand off exists, every sign in wall goes to the queue that way (owner, 2026-10-19). A wall is a page with a credential box, a sign in's or a sign up's: a box that takes typed text, which the browser reads as a password, a one time code or the user name of a login form (SPEC-browser's `credentials`), or whose label names a password or a sign in code. A check box, a yes or no group, a drop down list, a file field or a widget the app does not fill (a radio group, a list box) is never one, whatever its label: it is kept, shown, answered and refilled as any other of its kind. A typing the browser refuses as a credential input (`credential`) makes its page a wall too: the fill stops there as `needs_person: signin`. No credential box is ever a field of the application, recorded, shown, answered, refilled or typed by the app, and a page that holds one is a wall (Greenhouse's own e-mail code aside, which the person types on the page), never a form left to answer or approve.
- The person's persisted browser profile keeps the session, so the next application on that site is already connected. An expired session is the same hand off again.
- Accepting an ATS account's terms is the person's act, made by him in the live view.

## Complete my profile on a site (owner decision 3)

The person asks the agent to fill the candidate profile that a site keeps (Workday's "My Information" and "My Experience" pages, LinkedIn's profile sections, a company portal) from the app's profile, so later applications there start from a complete profile. It is never started by the app on its own, never part of Full auto, and it is not an application: no posting changes.
1. **Start:** the person gives the site (an address, or the shortcut offered after a "Needs you: sign in" notice, or LinkedIn from Account when the opt in is on). Refusals before anything is spent: LinkedIn without the opt in; an address that is not http or https. A `profile_fill` lease (flagged `profile_edit`) on the person's profile; a sign in wall opens the hand off.
2. **Read and fill, section by section:** the agent reads the site's profile page (contact, summary, experience, education, skills, languages, links, résumé) and fills each section **from the sources of "Answers to standard questions"**: identity, `experience`, `education`, `skills`, `languages`, links, `answers`, and the résumé from the person's reference CV (the version rendered by the documents module). A field with no source is left. A value already on the site that differs from the profile is shown as a difference and kept unless the person ticks "Replace". Typing goes through the paced tools.
3. **Review before saving, where the site allows:** per section, a screenshot and a table (field, value to write, source, value now on the site). The person edits a value or approves the section (`profile_section` authorisation, bound to the hash of that section's values, used once, valid 24 hours); then **the app's code clicks that section's Save** (`profile_gate.py`, the second and last caller of the browser's `gate_click`), after the agent has named the Save element with `designate_submit` and the screenshot has highlighted it. A site that saves as you type has no Save to review: the person is told before the run ("This site saves as you type, so a section cannot be reviewed before it is written."), confirms, and the run then writes **empty fields only** and never overwrites a value.
4. **Never, on a site profile:** change the e-mail, the password, a phone verification or any security setting; change privacy, visibility, "open to work" or recruiter contact settings; tick notification, marketing, talent community or consent boxes; fill diversity forms beyond the person's stored `eeo_policy`; invent a date, a title, a number or a skill (rule: every value traces to the profile, checked by `factcheck_rules.check`); delete or withdraw anything; accept terms; follow instructions in a page.
5. **Report:** per section, the screenshot before and after the save, the fields written with their sources, what was left; the run is `done` when every section is saved, skipped or left, or `cancelled`. Pace: on LinkedIn the pacing of SPEC-browser applies; a checkpoint page stops LinkedIn work for 7 days.

## Answers to standard questions

- **Sources, in this order:** (1) the identity section (names, e-mail, phone, city and country, links, languages); (2) the documents (the CV and letter PDFs, the letter's text for a cover letter box); (3) the `answers` section of the profile; (4) the person's **answer bank**: answers explicitly saved in Settings' Saved answers, or with the review's box "Remember this answer for next forms" (owner, 2026-11-05), are kept with their questions and reused when the same question comes back; application edits stay local (owner, 2026-10-23); (5) the agent, only for what is left, and only from the profile, the posting and the documents.
- **Profile fields** (the `answers` section has today: work authorisation per country with sponsorship, notice period, earliest start, salary policy, relocation, travel, motivation, how they heard, what to leave blank). This module adds, all optional: preferred name, pronouns (empty by default), years of experience as the person states them, highest degree and graduation year, willingness to work on site or hybrid, the default answer to "previously applied or worked here" (computed from the app's own applications first), consent to background and reference checks (a person's choice, never a default), the EEO policy, and the salary stance. `salary_policy` already exists on main as a free paragraph (`AnswersSection.salary_policy`) and stays untouched, valid as the sentence form: the stance is a new optional field (`leave_blank`, `range`, `sentence`), defaulting to `sentence` for a profile whose paragraph is filled and to `leave_blank` otherwise, and the ranges are a second new field. The owner's answers stay his: nothing of the parent folder's practicalities is copied into the app; he fills his profile or imports it.
- **Work authorisation** is answered from the person's list for the country of the posting's location (the posting's location parsed by the jobs module's gazetteer); a country not in the list is left.
- **EEO questions** (gender, ethnicity, race, disability, veteran status, sexual orientation, age): the policy `decline` by default chooses "I don't wish to answer" or its equivalent when the form offers it and leaves the field otherwise. An actual answer is given only if the person stored that exact answer in the profile. Never inferred.
- **Salary:** the salary stance decides: `leave_blank` (default), a range the person wrote per currency, or the sentence the person wrote in `salary_policy`. A required salary field with no policy that fits is left, so it goes to the person. Never a figure the person did not write.
- **Free text** ("Why do you want to join?", "Tell us about a project"): written by the agent from the person's motivation, facts and the letter, in the posting's language, checked by the documents module's fact check rules (mail M9's `factcheck_rules.check`). An issue blocks Full auto and is shown in review.

## What the agent may never do

One click Apply's agent (A19, owner 2026-11-05: "no guardrails") keeps of this list only hard rules 2, 3 and 7, the emergency stop and no second sending ([SPEC-browser](SPEC-browser.md#one-click-applys-lease-a19)).

- **Invent an answer:** a required question whose answer the sources do not hold is left, never guessed. A yes or no about a skill or a number of years is answered yes only when the profile proves it.
- **Accept terms beyond the application itself.** Allowed: the employer's privacy notice for processing this application, and "I confirm this information is accurate" (true by construction: every value has a source). Never ticked by the agent: arbitration agreements, marketing, newsletters, talent communities, retention beyond the application, background check authorisations, any other legal commitment; in Full auto a required box of that kind sends the application to the person.
- **Pay**, or enter a card: a page asking for payment ends the attempt with `payment_requested` and the run report says "This site asked for a payment. Applications are free; this may not be a genuine posting."
- **Create an account, choose a password, or type one** (owner decision 3): sign ins and sign ups are the person's, made in the live view in a session without automation.
- **Click Submit**, delete, withdraw, or navigate outside the hosts of the lease.
- **Follow instructions found in a page.** Posting and form text is data; every apply agent's system prompt says so, and a form that addresses "AI assistants" is reported in the run report.

## Guardrails, checked by the gate in every mode where the app clicks

The decision is one transaction, under the posting's row lock and a per person apply lock, with the checks in this order, and it writes its audit row before any click, whatever the outcome:
1. **Kill switches:** the setting `APPLY_SUBMIT_ENABLED` and the administrator's pause (`./deploy.sh apply pause`, a Redis flag) stop every submission at once, checked again just before the click; the person's own "Pause automatic applications" stops Full auto and pending approvals (checked with the caps, step 5, at the decision and again before the click).
2. **The authorisation:** its kind, owner, application, the hash of answers and documents, unused, not expired.
3. **Never apply rules:** the person's `never_apply` rules on the company and the title, as the sweep reads them (`sweep.never_apply_rules`), with the text folded (accents, case, compatibility forms, characters a reader does not see such as a soft hyphen or a zero width space, and Cyrillic, Greek and small capital letters drawn like Latin ones) and a term also matched at the start or the end of a glued word, so "cyber" refuses "Cybersecurity Engineer" (`apply/guardrails.py`, A2); and the sweep's `never_apply` event. Rules that no longer validate refuse as a match would. These are the person's own rules only: the app has no rule of its own about a kind of role (owner, 2026-11-05; the exclusion of cybersecurity roles is one of the owner's own rules, in his profile). As in mail decision 12, the rule stops what the AI does by itself: the agent never starts filling such a posting, and the gate refuses any authorisation. Apply myself stays possible, with the warning "Your rules exclude this role." In Full auto, a never apply term in the first 1,500 characters of the posting's text also sends it to the review queue.
4. **Duplicates:** refused when the posting already has an application `submitted` or `submit_unknown`; when another posting of the person has one for the same company and the same normalised title in the last 180 days (the same role found on two boards; a posting marked applied without a date counts, since nothing shows it is older); when the canonical form address (ATS and its job id) was already used (Apply myself only warns, `same_address`, when the address is an ordinary one, not an ATS job, and the other posting is only marked applied); when the posting's status is `applied` or later. A skipped (passed) posting is refused with `passed` in Review first and Full auto at start, at the decision and at both submission rechecks; Apply myself only warns, and other duplicate refusals still apply. Undo the Pass first to let the app apply (#528, owner, 2026-10-21).
5. **Caps:** the person's company caps (`apply_company_caps`: company, at most N applications in D days, and a cool down for the same role, entered by the person; for instance "Example Robotics: 3 in 90 days, 180 days before the same role again"), counted on `submitted` and `submit_unknown` rows; Full auto's one per company rule (owner decision 1) and its safety ceiling of 30 submissions a day (owner decision 2); the pace per ATS host across all people (one submission every 3 minutes, 20 in any 24 hours, the Jetson's one address; LinkedIn: one every 10 minutes, 15 in any 24 hours): a host not free yet keeps an approved application waiting, with the time it goes, and nothing is refused. Every cap refusal leaves the application `ready`.
6. **Documents and text:** the gate reads `documents.current` and checks that the versions it returns are the ones the approval's hash covers (a newer approved version written since the approval makes the hash stale: 409 and the current fields shown). It refuses nothing on a document's content: the documents module never blocks, and `documents.freeze` never refuses. The gate does not freeze; the freeze is called only in the transaction that records the submission (step 5 of the flow). In Full auto, apply's own rule: the item goes to the review queue when a version overflows its page or its `corrections` list is not empty, when the agent's free text has a fact check issue (`factcheck_rules.check`), or when the check by model did not pass. `submit_unknown` freezes the sent versions as well, in the transaction that records it, because the files may have been received.
7. **Full auto only:** the posting's fit at or above the person's `min_fit`, a supported ATS, no field left, no captcha seen during the fill, the rehearsals done, and the check by model passed (A13c, `model_check.py`: one call per item with free text written by the agent, before the decision; without a stored `passed` for the current answers, after a flag, a failed run or no credential, the item goes to the review queue with its reason). The hold comes after the decision: the submit lease checks that it elapsed, that Full auto is still on and the Like still valid, with the kill switches, the caps and the read back, just before the click.
8. **LinkedIn only:** the person's acceptance of the current LinkedIn sentence is recorded and no checkpoint pause is active.
A refusal in Full auto moves the application to the review queue with the reason, never to the bin. Every Full auto item sent to the review queue (a refusal of the decision or of the submit lease's recheck, a sign in wall, a held item when Full auto is turned off, a rehearsal answered Yes) becomes a `review` item in the same transaction, so the person's Approve writes a `person_approval` the gate accepts (A13a).

## Audit trail and run report

- `application_events`: every step with its time and code (created, refused with code, lease, filled counts, left, screenshot, approval, authorisation, decision, click, confirmation seen or not, status changed, cancelled, kill switch), ids and counts only; the answers themselves live in `application_answers` (label, kind, value, source, required) and the images in `application_screenshots` (JPEG, at most 2 MB each, "before" and "after" kept as long as the application; step and error images 30 days).
- The run report on the job page: the timeline, the fields with their sources (profile, document, answer bank, agent, person, left), the two screenshots, the cost in tokens, the duration, and what the person must do if anything.
- Nothing of an answer or a screenshot goes into logs, errors, job arguments or arq results (the canary of mail M1, reused).

## Failures and retries

- Before the click: a timeout, a 5xx page, a browser crash: up to 2 retries, after 10 and 60 minutes, then `failed`. A missing selector of an adapter during the fill (`form_changed`): the generic form agent takes over by itself in both modes (owner, 2026-10-19), within the run's token budget and every guardrail; in Full auto the item is submitted only when every Full auto check passes, the check by model included, else it goes to the review queue. The submit lease's read back keeps its own rule (no model, back to `ready`).
- Owner decision of 2026-10-21 (#560): a 429 is retried by the same rule; a 403 is not retried and waits as `needs_person: forbidden`, with the page in the live view. A detected captcha, sign in, e-mail code or payment step takes precedence over the HTTP status.
- `needs_person` (a captcha, a sign in or sign up wall, which is the hand off of SPEC-browser, a left field, an e-mail code with no mail connected): the person is told on the job page and in the queue, the live view opens on the tab; after 30 minutes without them the lease ends and the application waits in the queue.
- After the click: a confirmation (the adapter's patterns, `stage.py`'s `SUCCESS` as the base) is `submitted`; the form's errors are `refused_by_site` (the fields named, nothing resubmitted; in Review first the person fixes and approves again); no answer in 60 s is `submit_unknown` (the sent versions are frozen too, see guardrail 6).
- A posting that answers "no longer accepting applications", 404 or 410: `posting_closed`, and a proposal to move the posting to `closed`.

## Cost per application

The model is chosen by the credential (`model_by_credential`) and each run carries an estimate and a ceiling of twice that (`token_budget`), priced by ingestion's `pricing.py`. Estimates to be measured in A5 and A8:
- **Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever with a known adapter:** no model for standard fields; one call for the unrecognised questions and the free text (about 15,000 tokens in, 2,000 out) and, in Full auto, one check by model of that text (about 6,000 in, 500 out; apply's own call, A13c). About 25,000 tokens per application.
- **The generic strategy:** an agent loop over snapshots (8,000 to 20,000 tokens each), at most 30 turns: 150,000 to 400,000 tokens in (mostly cache reads), 10,000 out.
- **The submit lease after an approval:** no model.
- **Complete my profile on a site:** one model call per section, about 10,000 tokens in and 1,500 out on a site with an adapter (Workday, LinkedIn), so about 50,000 tokens for a five section profile; an unknown site uses the generic agent loop, capped as above. Measured in A18a.
Automatic runs share the person's daily cap of automatic runs and the person's active agent runs (`runs.ACTIVE_PER_USER`); the answer agent's run executes inside the fill, in the worker's process, outside the agents queue's two concurrent runs, so the worker holds at most four Claude Code processes (two fills at once with BROWSER_SLOTS 2: [SPEC-browser, Two browsers at once](SPEC-browser.md#two-browsers-at-once-owner-2026-11-06)); a run without a credential falls back to deterministic filling and leaves the rest.

Saved answers start empty. POST accepts only `{question, answer}`, with a question of 1 to 200
characters and an answer of 1 to 5000 characters. The server trims surrounding whitespace,
preserves Unicode and LF answer line breaks, and refuses empty values, other control or format
characters and questions without a normalized key. The session supplies the owner and the
server derives the question key. Creation returns 201 with the current list. A duplicate
normalized question, including a concurrent creation, returns 409 `saved_question_exists`
without replacing the existing answer. PUT keeps its existing edit and forget semantics,
including 404 for an absent key. `used_count` counts verified fills once per application field;
unverified fills and local application edits do not count. A refill of the same application
field does not count again. Current identity, documents and profile answers precede the bank;
a profile value that cannot fit the offered choices stays left.

The document-letter allowance is persisted by the adapter, survives a person edit and a refill of the same field shape, and cannot be granted by an answer request, label or field key. Existing rows without that provenance keep the ordinary limit until a fresh fill establishes it. Approval answers expose the server-derived `max_chars` and a `left_reason` when the document letter could not be typed. A letter over 10000 characters, or with a tab, carriage return or another refused control character, is left empty with its reason, never shortened or silently normalised. LF line breaks are allowed. The answer route accepts at most 121024 bytes, including JSON surrogate escapes for 10000 astral characters. The native editor counts UTF-16 units, so some symbols use two of its 5000 or 10000 positions; its counter uses that same count. Backend and browser typing limits count Unicode code points. A saved answer is displayed whole even when it exceeds the native editor limit. Email application subjects use a separate limit of 998 Unicode code points and reject CR or LF before a write; their approval view exposes `max_chars: 998`. An invalid legacy subject remains visible and editable, and approval returns a typed 422 instead of starting a send.

## Testing strategy

- Unit: the answer resolver per field kind and source, the EEO and salary policies, the term box classifier, the duplicate and cap rules, the authorisation and its hash, the state machine.
- Integration: a fake ATS server (`tests/ats_forms_stub.py`, invented companies) serving Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday like, generic and e-mail postings with captcha, error and slow variants, recording what is submitted; the real browser container; the fake model backend. Every task proves that a submission reaches the fake only through the gate, and that person B cannot read or approve A's application.
- Journeys on the Mac stack; on the Jetson the owner's checkpoint.
- **No test ever submits to a real company.**

## Boundaries

- **Always:** the gate before any click by the app; screenshots before and after; the person can watch, take over, cancel and pause.
- **Ask first:** a new ATS in Full auto; raising a cap's default or the safety ceiling's default; any captcha service.
- **Never:** a model clicking Submit in Review first or Full auto, or a site profile's Save; the app choosing a password or creating an account; an invented answer or an invented profile entry; a ticked arbitration or marketing box; a payment; a second submission after `submit_unknown` by the app itself (in One click Apply only the person's Try again runs the agent again, which looks on the site first); a submission to a never apply role by the AI.

## Success criteria

1. On the Jetson, the owner clicks Apply on a real Greenhouse or Ashby posting he chose, watches the form fill, approves, and the posting is `applied` with both screenshots and the frozen documents.
2. The review queue holds the applications of his Likes. An answer edited there stays local to that application; an answer separately saved in Saved answers is reused when the same question comes back (owner, 2026-10-23).
3. In Full auto, rehearsals, then real submissions, stay within the fit threshold, the one per company rule and the safety ceiling; a captcha or a left field lands in the queue; the pause switch stops everything at once (tests and one manual check).
4. On a site that needs an account, the run waits, the owner signs in himself in the live view, the run continues, and the next day's application there is already signed in.
5. The owner asks the agent to complete his profile on a site, reviews a section, approves it, and finds it saved with only values from his profile.
6. Tests prove: no submission outside the gate, no duplicate, no never apply role, no cross person access.
