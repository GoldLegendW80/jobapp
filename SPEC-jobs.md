# Spec: jobs

Module of [CAPABILITY-MAP.md](CAPABILITY-MAP.md). Depends on `profile`. `documents`, `agents` and `apply` act on its rows.

## Objective

Every user has their own job pipeline in the app: the postings found, their fit score against the user's profile, their status from `found` to `applied` and beyond, and notes. It replaces `data/jobs.csv`, the `data/jobs/<id>/` folders and the `jobs.html` dashboard, and the `find` and `score` commands of `engine/jobs.py`.

User stories:
- As Harald, I find my 433 existing rows in the app with their status, fit, competencies, gaps, notes, dates, posting text and the files I already sent (CV and letter PDFs, answers), imported once from `../data`.
- As a user, I see a dashboard: counts per status, a fit histogram, and a list I can search, filter (place, work mode, contract type, website, published date, pay, fit range, application, status, found date, company, the search that added them) and sort; one row opens a detail view with the posting text and everything attached.
- As a user, I select one or many rows and change their status or add a note in one action; the history of every change is kept.
- As a user, I add a posting by hand (URL, company, title, location, text) when it comes from a site with no API.
- As a user, I press "Find jobs" and choose my websites and criteria for [interactive search](#interactive-search-626). Every search, manual or scheduled, runs the ticked websites; the automatic schedule (days, times, time zone) stays opt in, off until enabled. The API board sweeps (Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever) and the freelance feeds were deleted (owner, 2026-10-28).
- As a user, I see my postings on a map, as the current dashboard does.

## Scoring

Port of `engine/jobs.py score_row` against the user's `skills` section, using `api/app/profile/matching.py` for term matching (P2 made it the single source of matching rules). Output: fit 0 to 100, competencies named, gaps. A manual fit override is kept (`fit_overridden`) and never replaced by a rescore unless the user asks. **Rescored by itself after a skills change** (owner, product audit chapter 2, answer 4 of 2026-11-05): whoever changes the skills section (the Skills tab, an import, an accepted proposal), the profile's write asks for a rescore in its own transaction (`job_rescores`, `app/jobs/rescore.py`); the worker's `rescore_tick` (every 10 s, sweeps queue) runs it, one person at a time and never two of one person at once, and a change saved during a rescore runs one more after it (changes saved meanwhile make that one run). It counts the fits that reached the documents threshold (`search.documents.min_fit`) and those that fell under it; the Skills tab says them after the save ("4 more postings now fit you at 70 or more", with a link to `/jobs?fit_min=70&sort=fit`), and says before the save what the change may cost in documents (SPEC-documents, Automatic generation). "Rescore all postings" in the Jobs menu stays for a manual rescore. A golden test proves the port gives the same fit, competencies and gaps as the engine on every row of `../data/jobs.csv` that has a posting.

## Before the click (owner, 2026-11-07, "build all" of the career-ops analysis)

What a posting's text says that may decide whether the person can take it, read at store (a sweep, a posting added by hand, an import, and again when an import changes its text) and at every rescore by pure rules in English and French (`app/jobs/signals.py`, migration 0038): `signals` holds each signal with the posting's own sentence (it does not sponsor a visa, it does, it asks for the right to work in a place, a degree, an independent contractor, a fixed term, unpaid work, a fee the candidate pays, the closing date); `closes_on` the closing date it states in an unambiguous form with its year, never guessed; `text_fingerprint` a 64-bit SimHash of its word pairs. `app/jobs/notices.py` turns them into sentences of facts against the person's own profile and rows: the work authorisation of `answers` for the posting's country or the place it names ("This posting says it does not sponsor visas, and your profile says you need sponsorship in Germany", or that it says nothing about sponsorship where the profile needs it), a stated pay under `search.compensation` in the same currency and period, the closing date, a posting of another company whose fingerprint is at most 5 of 64 bits away (career-ops's 92 %), and the person's applications to the same company (folded as apply's duplicate rule folds), with their dates and outcomes. `GET /api/jobs/{id}` answers them as `notices` (`{kind, code, text, quote, level, job_id}`, the warnings first); the posting page shows them under its header, the Discover card and the Apply confirmation too. Information only, never a refusal or a hidden action (A19, A20; the app adds no rule of its own about a kind of role, 2026-11-05); the years a posting asks stay the score's gap ("5+ years"). A row stored before 0038 is read on the fly until a rescore stores it.

How the rules read a sentence, each an allow list where the rest gives nothing: the right to work is asked only by a sentence that requires it, the person's state ("eligible to work", "autorisé à travailler"), a right with requirement wording (must, required, need to have or hold, valid, only candidates with; devez, doit, requis, exigé, obligatoire, indispensable) or a right alone as a list item ("Right to work in the UK"), and every place of a list ("in the UK or the EU" gives both); never when the employer sponsors, gives, handles or arranges it ("We'll handle your work permit for Germany", "Nous prenons en charge votre permis de travail"), anywhere in the sentence: as its object ("we arrange it", "nous nous en chargeons"), in a relative clause ("which we can provide", "que nous fournissons") or as the agent of a passive ("sponsored by the company", "pris en charge par l'entreprise", "obtenu par nos soins"; a person as the agent, "provided by the candidate", keeps the requirement), the employer being "we", "nous" or a common noun for it (the company, the firm, the organisation, the team, l'entreprise, l'employeur, notre équipe and the like). Declared residue: an employer named only by its own name or by a noun the reading does not know, or a giving verb it does not know, still reads as a requirement; the notice quotes the sentence, so the person sees it. Also not when the sentence reads as sponsorship, or when it negates the requirement ("No work permit for Germany is required"), while "authorised to work in Canada without sponsorship" stays a requirement. A refusal of sponsorship allows only known words between its negation and its verb ("do not currently sponsor"), so "We can't wait to sponsor your visa" or "We don't just sponsor visas" refuse nothing. DUT and BTS are a degree only beside a French word of diplomas (titulaire, diplômé, niveau, requis...), never "device under test"; "en freelance" is a contract only of the role ("Mission en freelance"), never "avec des développeurs en freelance"; a closing date follows applications, the posting or the role ("This role closes on"), or a label that opens its sentence ("Closes:", "Deadline:", "Open until"), never "The office closes on 24 December". Places are read longest first, so Latin, South, Central and North America are regions of their own, never the United States.

## Assess this posting (owner, 2026-11-07)

From "build all" of the career-ops analysis (item 9; adapted from career-ops's evaluation and `role-clarity.mjs`, MIT, credited in NOTICE). On the posting's page, Assess (in the side rail, under the documents) starts the agent `jobs_assess` on the person's own credential, on that click only (D29): `POST /api/jobs/{id}/assess` answers 202 with the queued run, or with the run already under way on that posting (a second click starts nothing); 404, 409 without a credential, 429 and 503 as POST /api/agents/runs. The agent is internal (only this route starts it, Admin, Agents lists it under Jobs and chat), reads its own posting (`get_job`, bound to the run's job) and the profile (`get_profile_section`), never searches the web, and submits once through `submit_assessment`, whose strict schema and checks are code (`app/jobs/assess.py`): at most 12 requirements, each with an importance band (critical, high, meaningful, preferred, low signal), an evidence tier (stated with a quote of the posting, structural with where the posting carries it, inferred), the person's match and its evidence; an inferred requirement is never critical or high; every critical or high gap carries its interview risk and a mitigation; the culture screen against the person's own criteria (the search section and the facts), never the agent's; the signals the text shows (specificity, contradiction, contractor wording, AI wording beyond the role, fixed term); a one sentence verdict and how sure it is. Every quote must be in the posting, word for word (case, spaces, typographic quotes and the punctuation around it aside; an elision "..." may join two pieces in order); code sorts the rows (importance, then unmet first), applies the dash rule to what the model wrote, and adds the role clarity points, read by code from the posting (`app/jobs/role_clarity.py`, English and French: who the role reports to, what it owns, what it delivers, how success is measured early on, who it works with, how often it delivers; the questions to ask when three or more are missing; a text in another language is not read). The result replaces the posting's last assessment in `job_assessments(job_id pk, user_id, fk (job_id, user_id) to jobs cascade, run_id fk agent_runs set null, created_at, result jsonb)` (migration 0044); `GET /api/jobs/{id}/assessment` answers it with the latest run. The rail shows the verdict, how sure, the gaps to prepare and the keyword fit side by side; the whole assessment is folded under the posting. The assessment never changes the fit, which stays the deck's sort key. Not in this round: the CV and letter writers and the hiring panel do not read the assessment yet (a follow up).

## Sweep

**Anywhere on the web (jobs J21, #807).** Every sweep (manual, scheduled, onboarding) searches the whole web after the websites unless the person unticked "Anywhere on the web" (`search.web_search`, true when absent): the `web_discovery` agent gets every saved criterion and the profile summary, makes WebSearch calls with no cap of the app's (Claude Code's own default bound of 200 a session, `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_WEB_SEARCHES_PER_SESSION ?? 200`, as on the Mac), holds the person's browser only around its page calls, opens or fetches each promising posting and reports it with its own address; its stats entry has mode `web`, and its postings are deduplicated with every other source's by address or by company, title and place.

**How widely the web source searches (jobs J23, #813).** Before `report_no_postings` is accepted, the web source searched its plan: for each wanted place (none saved, or only a place that says no limit: one row per search language instead, the profile's French or English else both, its roles named in that language, French with France's boards), the saved titles (the first 12), at least three roles next to the profile (`app/browser/web_plan.py`, ROLES read against the summary's recent roles, skills and adjacent skills, then the broad "développeur", "data", "intelligence artificielle"), and each national public job board of the place's country by a `site:` query (France and its overseas places: candidat.francetravail.fr, apec.fr, hellowork.com; a small table for Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg, the United Kingdom, Germany, Canada, Spain and the United States; another country: a query of the place and a word for jobs). A family counts once a WebSearch query names the place (as written, a part of it, LinkedIn's name and its aliases, the département number, its main towns: Saint-Denis, Saint-Pierre, Le Port, Saint-Paul for La Réunion, Pointe-à-Pitre, Les Abymes, Baie-Mahault, Basse-Terre for Guadeloupe, Papeete, Faa'a, Punaauia for French Polynesia, a town another place has too only right next to a postcode of its place, "Saint-Denis 97400", never alone, round 2 of #814) and the family, whole words, case and accents aside, exclusions aside, role forms longest first with each word counted once ("data scientist" is one role, not also "data"). Words side by side are ANDed by a search engine, so a query without OR (` OR ` or `|`) counts for one title, one role and one board at most, for no board with two `site:` operators, and for no place when it names the places of two rows (round 1 of #814). A query counts once WebSearch answered it without an error; a refusal of the app's gate (a spent cap) is no error. Titles and places no query can name ("++", "!!!") are not planned, and titles that fold to the same words ("ingénieur IA", "Ingenieur IA") are planned once. The plan is shown in the agent's first message (`search_plan`), the refusal names what is left; WebSearch not offered, two errors in a row or a stopped session (lease ended, permission taken back, cancel) lift it, and a plan WebSearch's errors lifted before it was searched ends the source `web_search_failed`, never searched with nothing new. A posting with no exact date is kept: the page's own rough wording (`date_shown`, "il y a un mois") when its words are on the page, and the first search event's `discovery.date` says `exact`, `approximate` or `unknown`, shown on the job's timeline ("Found by a search, dated "il y a un mois" on the posting (approximate date)", or "Found by a search, the posting gives no date"); only a date clearly older than the saved window is left by the agent.

**What the search agents report (jobs J24, #815, owner B21).** The web source and every website's agent report each open posting in a wanted place (or remote when wanted) whose role matches a saved title OR the profile's skills or a role next to them (J23's role table), whatever its title wording, seniority or years asked: a full stack or data engineer posting for a GenAI profile with full stack and data skills, a posting asking 5 years of a 3.5 years profile. Years, seniority, pay and a partial skill match never leave a posting: scoring and the posting reader compute fit and gaps ("5+ years") and the person sees them. A posting is left only in another place, closed or expired, clearly older than the window, of an unwanted title or contract, excluded by a never apply rule, or not a job posting (for the contract, the work mode, the age and the never apply rules, replaced by jobs J30: [Sweep](#sweep), "Kept with a badge"). A posting known only from a search result or a blocking aggregator (Indeed, Mitula) is looked for on the employer's site, France Travail or another public board, and that page reported. In code, verification, the sources' sort and the store never read the saved titles: only unwanted titles and places drop a posting and the never apply rules skip one (`api/tests/jobs/test_j24_report_what_fits.py`; other contracts too until jobs J30).

**The town and AI boards searches of each place (jobs J25, #817).** Beside J23's families, each wanted place's plan holds two more, each searched by a query of its own (a query naming no other place of the plan; an OR inside it still counts), and one single query never serves both: `town`, a query naming the place's main town (the first town of the plan's town table: Saint-Denis for La Réunion, Pointe-à-Pitre for Guadeloupe, Papeete for Tahiti; the town the place names itself, "Saint-Pierre, La Réunion"; a place without a town table needs none), and `ai_boards`, a `site:` query of at least one niche AI and tech job board naming the place or its main town (several boards joined by OR in one query count too; two `site:` with no OR count for nothing), public job boards only (The Intelligence Academy, Hellowork, Welcome to the Jungle, Free-Work, LesJeudis, Choose Your Boss, AI Jobs; Hellowork, a national board too, counts here only in a query naming the main town). An offer phrase search without `site:` ("offre emploi genai engineer Saint-Denis La Réunion") is no `ai_boards` query (jobs J26, #819: the search engine returned none of the niche board's pages). The plan shown names each place's `main_town`, its `ai_boards` and an `ai_boards_query` ("site:the-intelligence-academy.com OR site:hellowork.com data Saint-Denis La Réunion"); the refusal names the boards and that example. With no place saved, neither is needed.

**Clean titles and one row per job across boards (jobs J25, #817).** Before a verified posting is stored and compared, its title loses its gender markers (H/F, F/H, (H/F), M/F, M/W/D, F/H/X: an allow list of letter sets, so "C/C++" stays), a leading offer phrase, a trailing board name (one whose name is a title's word, Talent.com, only with its domain), and a trailing name that is the posting's company and more than words of a title or a qualifier ("Chef de projet - IA" at "IA Solutions" keeps "IA"); with no employer stated, a name becomes the company only on positive evidence: after the last marker a recruiter, or an organisation word (SAS, SARL, groupe, analytics, consulting, conseil, solutions, technologies, recrutement...) beside a word of its own name, not a place and no word of a title; after a separator a recruiter only. Anything else stays ("Chef de projet Data & IA", "Développeur Java H/F Expérimenté", "Stage Data Scientist H/F 6 mois", "Data Engineer (H/F) Anglais courant"). Contract markers go too (jobs J26, #819): CDI, CDD, Stage, Alternance, Freelance, Intérim or VIE (any case, VIE in capitals only), alone or joined ("CDI ou CDD"), with a duration or not ("CDD 6 mois"), in parentheses or brackets, as a whole part between separators or the title's edges ("Data scientist - (cdi)", "CDI - Data Scientist"), as an "en CDI" or "en CDD" tail, or as a tail with its duration; the contract type keeps what they said, and a contract word beside the title's own words stays ("Gestionnaire CDD", "Responsable des contrats CDI", "Stage Manager", "Actuaire (Vie)"). The title as shown stays in the posting's text and in the discovery's `title_shown`; a company taken from the title is `company_from_title`, and a recruitment agency (a table of agencies, or a word such as "recruitment" or "intérim"; an agency whose bare name is a common word, Spring, Actual, Crit, Fed, only with the rest of its name: "Actual Group") is flagged `recruiter`. Beside the address and the exact employer, title and location rule, a posting is a duplicate of a row found within 60 days with the same employer (its generic words aside: "LHH Recruitment Solutions" is "LHH"), the same cleaned title and the same place (the town and the département or overseas region it names: "Saint-Denis (974)" is "Saint-Denis, La Réunion", never "Saint-Denis (93)"; a town several départements have, Saint-Denis or Saint-Pierre, is no known place without its département), compared with the newest row of that key: one row naming both sources, the first address kept. With no employer stated or no place known, only the address joins two postings. Stored rows are compared as they are and as their title cleans (a row stored before J25 as "Data Scientist H/F" still matches the exact rule), and never edited.

**The web search finishes its plan (jobs J27, #821).** A posting kept spares nothing of the web source's plan (J23, J25, J26): every `report_postings` answer names what is left (`plan_left`) and says to go on, and `report_no_postings` ("no_match": no more postings fit, whatever was reported) is refused with what is left until the plan is searched, a stopped session, WebSearch not offered or failing twice in a row aside. When the agent ends its turn without `report_no_postings` while the plan is left, the runner continues the same Claude Code session with a follow up message naming what is left (`AgentDefinition.follow_up`, at most `runner.FOLLOW_UPS_MAX` 2 a run, each recorded as a `follow_up` event: "Asked to go on (1 of 2)"); still left after them, the run ends (an `unfinished` event: "Ended with its plan unfinished") and the source ends `plan_unfinished`, partial when it found postings (kept), never retried, said in the Jobs summary. A turn ended with the plan searched ends the run. The website search (B27) keeps its rule: its routes are needed only before a website ends with nothing.

**The web search opens the niche board results in a wanted place (jobs J28, #823).** From each WebSearch answer of a web run (its `Links:` results, title and address, told by the runner to the definition), code keeps the results whose host is a niche AI board of the plan (`web_plan.NICHE` or a subdomain of one) and whose title or address path names a wanted place by the rules a query names it by (its names, its département number, its towns no other place has, a shared town such as Saint-Denis only right next to a postcode of the place), or whose address is a job posting of its board (`web_plan.POSTING`: `/job-board/offre/<slug>` on the-intelligence-academy.com, `/<lang>/emplois/<id>.html` on Hellowork) when the WebSearch query that returned it names a wanted place by the same rules (round 1 of #824: the real results of that query name no place), the first 15 of the run in answer order (`web_plan.niche_link`, `FinderSession.note_niche_links`). `report_no_postings` is refused, naming their addresses (never a result's title, web text Activity would keep), while one is neither opened by the agent (web_fetch or browser_navigate, whatever the page answers) nor named by a `report_postings` item (kept or refused by the verification); every `report_postings` answer names those left (`niche_results_left`), the J27 follow up names them too, and a run ended with one left ends `plan_unfinished`. A stopped session, WebSearch not offered or failing twice in a row lift it as they lift the plan. Code fetches none of them: the agent opens them and judges each on its own page.

**Kept with a badge (jobs J30, #829).** Owner, 2026-11-26: "Je veux vraiment qu'on remonte un max de job"; for a posting in a wanted place that fits the profile but is outside a criterion (another contract, remote, older than the window), "Garder avec un badge". So the web source and every website's agent report a posting in a wanted place, or remote, that fits the profile whatever its contract, its work mode and its age (the prompts, the web plan's `report` line, the report tools' texts); they leave only a posting in another place, closed or expired, of an unwanted title or place, or not a job posting; a never apply rule still stores the posting as `skipped`. Code no longer drops another contract either (`interactive._Site._sort`, LinkedIn's public job search and its page reader after reading), and LinkedIn's public job search and its page no longer ask LinkedIn for the saved contract types (planner default: no `f_JT` filter, the age filter `f_TPR` kept, since that search has few requests and an old posting is mostly closed). What is outside the criteria is a badge computed by code, never taken from an agent (`app/jobs/criteria_badge.py`, `codes`), from the posting's own fields and the criteria of the search that found it, in this order: `contract`, a contract the posting states that the search's contract types do not hold (none saved: any; a CDI counts only when stated, `jobs.contract_stated`: a word of the title, location or address, a source's contract other than CDI, the posting reader's quote or the person's edit, since `importer.kind` answers CDI when nothing says otherwise; the contract a website has by nature, B21, when that website found it); `work_mode`, a work mode the posting states that the saved remote stance does not hold ("any" or none: any; planner default: on site holds on site and hybrid, since a hybrid job has days on site, remote holds remote only, hybrid holds hybrid only); `older`, a published date more than the search's days before `found_on` (planner default: from the day it was found, so a badge never changes with the calendar). A field the posting does not state gives no code. Then (jobs J35, #858) `language`: a language the posting requires that the person's profile does not list, against her current languages whatever the search (`app/jobs/languages.py`, `missing`; the rule follows the Language Gate of ai-job-search, NOTICE): the posting reader reads each language the role asks for (not the language the ad is written in), with its level as written, required or only welcome, the other languages accepted instead and its quote; a false badge being the costly error, every doubt gives a notice, never the badge: a language is required only when the reader says so and its quote (1) holds no welcome word ("a plus", "un atout", "von Vorteil", "erwünscht") and no negation ("no", "pas", "kein"), (2) offers no other language she lists as an alternative (one the reader may have split): a quote that names one holds no slash and no alternative word ("or", "oder", "ou", "bzw", "one of"), and only "and", "und", "et", "sowie", "ainsi que", a short link or another language's name stands between the two names, never nothing ("Fluent German and English" asks for both; "German or French", "German/English", "Bilingue français allemand", "Fluent English, German" stay a notice), (3) names the language as a language: a German stem only with an inflection or a skill glued to it ("Deutschkenntnisse", never "Deutschland"), the word before and after it each a clause's edge, a linking word, a level, a requirement word, a language noun or another language's name (so "the German market", "Deutsche Bank", "German speaking clients", "Pays basque" are not the language; "speaking" counts only on the person, "German-speaking candidate", "German speaking is a must"; a colon, or a dash standing alone, between a name and its level is a label ("German: fluent"); after "be", "are", "sind" or "êtes" a name with no level, requirement word or noun after it is a nationality, "Must be German"), never "Italien" nor "Corse" (also Italy in German and Corsica), "polish" only with its capital, and at a clause's start only before an edge, a level or a noun with a level close ("Polish the required UI flows" is a verb), and (4) holds a level or a requirement word ("required", "exigé", "zwingend", "Kenntnisse", "communication skills"; a verb of mastery, "maîtrisez", "sicher", is a level) within three words of it in its clause; welcome when the reader says it is not required and the quote holds a welcome word; unsure otherwise; names are a closed list of about eighty languages in their English, French, German and own spellings, sign languages as their own, a name outside it compared as written; the person's side also reads the names in Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch and Polish ("Inglés", "Tedesco") and the ISO 639 codes as a whole entry ("EN", "deu C1"), an entry that names no language ("N/A", "none", a dash) is no entry, and an entry it still cannot read turns the badge into a notice, since the asked language could be that one, and is named under the languages of her profile (`ProfileOut.languages_unread`); levels are read on one scale (CEFR A1 to C2, then native) from words in English, French and German, the person's at their lowest reading (her name's words when she wrote no level: "Anglais courant"), the posting's at their highest, a negation or words the scale does not know reading as unknown. ## Posting reader (owner, 2026-11-09, #764)

An agent reads each posting's text and fills its location, pay (min, max, currency, period), work mode, contract type, published date, company and a clean description (owner: "I want that it's an agent that create the jobs in the database"; [decisions](docs/decisions.md#interactive-job-search-2026-10-24-626)). Every source's postings are read (job websites, company boards, feeds, LinkedIn, alert e-mails).

- The agent `posting_reader` (`api/app/jobs/reader.py`, its prompt `prompts/reader.md`, its tools `reader_tools.py`) runs on the agents runtime as the ingestion agents do: internal, bound in code to at most 10 postings of one person (their ids in the run's input, checked to be hers and to hold a text), private (events hold tool names and sizes, never a value), on `claude-sonnet-5-5` for both kinds of credential. `read_postings` returns the bound postings by index with their text (at most 15,000 characters each) and the fields set by hand; `save_posting_facts` saves one posting's facts and takes an index, never an id.
- Grounding: each value comes with a quote of the posting text, found in it as the finder compares words (case, accents, spacing and punctuation aside); a value whose quote is not there is dropped and counted, never stored. Checks: a location of one line and at most 300 characters, a company of at most 200, the work mode and contract type of their enums, a published date in ISO form from 2000 to the day of the read, a pay of whole numbers (cents rounded by the agent) that `pay.stated_pay` accepts with a quote holding a digit.
- Clean description: the agent gives the first and last words (3 to 30) of the posting's own text; the text is cut from the first match of the first to the last match of the last after it, and a cut under 300 characters is refused. The cut is refused too when the agent was not shown the whole text, so no word it never saw is cut away: a text shortened to show it (over 15,000 characters, `posting_text_truncated`; the prompt tells it to leave the description out then) or changed since the run began (its digest in the run's binding); the posting keeps its whole text and its other facts are stored. The cut becomes the posting text, its first 300 characters the description, and the fit is computed again as a rescore does, a fit set by hand kept.
- Kept as they are: a field named in an `edit` event of Edit details (the four pay columns as one); a company or location that would make the posting the same as another of the person's; the contract of a website whose every posting has one by nature (the VIE website, B21). The reader writes what it changed as one `edit` event with `"via": "reader"` (History: "Read the posting and set the ..."), which never counts as set by hand, and `read_at`.
- During the sweep: once every source ended, the postings it stored are read in batches, one after the other in the sweep's own job (never on the agents queue), within 10 minutes and a minute before the sweep's own bound. One reading run at a time for the whole worker, whatever the number of sweeps (a slot every sweep's reading shares; a batch waiting for it counts no attempt), so the worker's Claude Code processes stay within [SPEC-browser's estimate](SPEC-browser.md#two-browsers-at-once-owner-2026-11-06) ("The fills"). Postings not read by then stay for the backlog; the reading never fails a sweep, without a credential it is skipped at once, and a run that failed for the credential, the provider or the worker ends it (`skipped`: `provider_failure`, `worker_failure`). `stats.reading` counts read, refused (values dropped), failed and left.
- The backlog: a cron of the agents queue each minute starts one run of up to 10 saved postings not read yet as its own `run_agent` job, one such run at a time for the whole app, for a person with a credential, no reading run and no sweep running, the person whose last reading is the oldest first. ## Liveness (owner, 2026-11-07)

Owner, 2026-11-07, "build all" of the career-ops analysis: a closed posting never gets documents or an application. The app reads the posting's own address (`jobs.url`) with one plain GET before the clicks that spend (Write, Apply, a Like, Apply to the selected postings) and before an apply run starts (SPEC-apply, `posting_closed`), reusing a check younger than an hour; "Check now" on the posting (`POST /api/jobs/{id}/check`) always asks again; and the worker's timer for the open postings (below, jobs J37). Never in a sweep. `api/app/jobs/liveness.py` reads the answer (pure functions, tables in English, French, German, Dutch, Spanish and Polish, rules adapted from career-ops's `liveness-core.mjs`, MIT, NOTICE); `api/app/jobs/liveness_check.py` makes the request and stores `{verdict, reason, sentence, checked_at, moved}` in `jobs.liveness` without moving `updated_at` (`moved`: this check moved the posting to closed; the posting says "so it moved to Closed" only then), only while the posting's address is still the one the check asked: a finding for an address the person changed meanwhile is dropped.

- **Closed by itself** only on a 404 or a 410 of the posting's own address, or a clause of its own page that says, whole, that this posting is closed, naming the posting, the page asked for or the employer ("Cette offre n'est plus disponible.", "This job is no longer available.", "We are no longer accepting applications.", "Applications closed for this role.", "Page not found"). A 404 on the page closes only next to its word ("Error 404", "404 Not Found", "404 error", "404 page"): a bare 404 is as often a phone number's area code ("Call (404) 555-0100"). A found, shortlisted, ready or staged posting then moves to `closed` (`set_status`, `via: "liveness"`); a click on it answers 409 `posting_closed` with the page's sentence quoted; nothing is deleted. A posting the app closed once and the person opened again is never closed again by the app: later findings only propose.
- **Proposed** (the "May be closed" badge on the posting and its Discover card, and "Mark closed", one click of the person): the same sentence on a page the address redirected to; a whole closure clause that names nothing ("Applications closed", "No longer accepting applications", "Candidatures closes", "Es werden keine Bewerbungen mehr angenommen."), which a "Similar jobs" list of the page may carry for another posting; closure words inside a longer clause that no time or condition word opens; a short badge ("Job expired", "Poste pourvu"); a listing page; an address that now leads elsewhere. Clicks go on.
- **Declared residue of the reading:** the page is read as plain text, without its regions, so a closure clause that names its posting ("This job is no longer available.") inside another posting's card still closes this one, and a nameless badge of such a list proposes it; a sentence about a non job offer that matches a closure ("This offer has expired.") closes too. The person reopens a posting the app closed, and the app never closes it again.
- **Unconfirmed, never closed**: any other status (401, 403, 429, 5xx), a robot check, a sign in page, too little text (a page its script draws), the network, the time, the pacer's store, a page the check cannot read (`unreadable`), any other error of the check (`check_failed`). A page whose charset is not a text encoding the check can use (`undefined`, `idna`, `base64`) is read as UTF-8. The check never raises: whatever stops it, the click goes on and the apply run starts. A LinkedIn posting is never asked, nor a redirect to LinkedIn followed: LinkedIn answers a plain GET with a sign in wall, and its requests belong to the LinkedIn checkbox, its pause and its cooldown.
- **The request**: http or https on ports 80, 443, 8080 or 8443, to a host whose every address is public (browserd's egress rule), connected to an address checked (the next public address of the host when one cannot be reached within 3 s), every redirect checked again (at most 4); no cookie, a User-Agent naming the app, the first 2 MB of the page, 8 s in all; each request, redirects included, paced with the browser's shared per host pacer of its own host (one request of a host at a time, 1 s after the previous one ended). Apply to the selected postings checks them side by side within 20 s: a posting not checked by then, or whose check fails, goes on as it is.
- **A new address**: an edit or an import that changes the posting's address (`jobs.url`) clears `jobs.liveness`, so the next click checks the new address instead of reusing the old one's finding.
- **The timer (jobs J37, plan #856 row 5; owner, 2026-11-26: "je veux aussi pouvoir mieux filtrer les annonces")**: the worker's cron `cron:liveness_timer` (sweeps queue, every minute, `api/app/jobs/liveness_timer.py`) checks by itself the pages of the person's `found` and `shortlisted` postings with an address, with the same check, request and rules as a click (`liveness_check.record`, so a closed page closes the posting with its History event, a posting the app closed once and the person reopened is only proposed, anything else changes no status, and an unreachable page is never a closed one). A posting the person acted on (CV ready, staged, applied and later) stays the clicks' and the apply run's: a finding that lands after she acted is stored and closes nothing. LinkedIn's postings are never asked and spend nothing. Order: first the postings a closing date proposes (below) whose page was not asked since, then those never checked (the day published, the day found when it gives none, oldest first), then the oldest check first; a posting checked less than `JOBS_LIVENESS_AGAIN_DAYS` (7) days ago is not due, unless its closing date proposes it. Bounds (settings, orchestrator defaults): at most `JOBS_LIVENESS_CHECKS_PER_TICK` (10) checks a tick in all, one after the other, the persons in turn; at most `JOBS_LIVENESS_CHECKS_PER_DAY` (150) of a person's postings checked in the last 24 hours, counted from the checks stored on them (`jobs.liveness.checked_at`, a click's included, a closing date's proposal not yet asked, a LinkedIn posting's and an address-less one's excluded: they asked nothing; a check whose finding the database refused is stored as `check_failed` in a transaction of its own, or else kept in the worker's memory for 7 days, and counts all the same), so the bound holds across restarts; one posting of a host a tick (`www.` and the bare name one host, a protected platform one host as the pacer keys it); a host whose answer to a check of anyone's posting in the last `JOBS_LIVENESS_HOST_REST_HOURS` (24) was a wall (`rate_limited`, `bot_check`, `refused`, `sign_in`) is left alone for every person, since every check leaves from the machine's one address; a host that a running search of anyone lists among its sources is left alone while it runs (the shared pacer only knows each page, not a search); each request is paced by the shared pacer, 1 s after the host's previous one ended, as a click's. One tick at a time whatever the number of workers: the tick holds a transaction advisory lock for its whole run, and a tick that finds it held does nothing; a dead worker's connection ends and its lock with it. A person with no open posting costs one shared query and no request. Cost: a list of 600 open postings takes 4 days to go round once (150 a day), then each is asked again after a week, about 86 requests a day.
- **A closing date that passed (jobs J37; orchestrator, planner default after the review of PR 869, round 1)**: a passed closing date never closes a posting by itself, since the review read false dates (a "Deadline:" or "Date limite :" label about a project's deadline, another posting's "Closes:" date on the same page, a deadline its own sentence keeps open with "until filled", a passed date in a posting a board still lists). Each tick, an open posting (`found`, `shortlisted`) whose closing date as its own text states it (`closes_on`, read by `signals.py` with its year) passed everywhere (the next day began at UTC-12), and on which no check was stored after that date, is proposed as maybe closed, with no request: `may_be_closed`, reason `closing_date`, the sentence the date was read from, so the "May be closed" badge and "Mark closed" show as for a page that may say so (the person closes it in one click, or keeps it), and the posting's words say "its closing date passed, so it may be closed". Its page is then asked first by the timer: a closed page closes it as any check does; a page that proposes gives its own words; a page with no sign of closing, or one the check cannot read, leaves the proposal standing with the page's answer (`page`, "; its page shows no sign of closing"), and that answer counts for the day and for a host's rest as any check's. The person's own Check now stores the page's answer, and the date proposes no more.
- **What the person sees (jobs J37)**: each closing's History line names why ("checked by the app: its page no longer exists", "its page says it is closed"); Insights ("Your search in numbers", `GET /api/insights` `closed_by_checks`) counts the postings the checks closed that stay closed and those of the last 7 days, and apart the open postings they propose as maybe closed, by their page and by their closing date. The counts lead to no list: no filter of the Jobs list holds the postings the app closed or proposes (`status=closed` also holds the person's own closings).
- **Not built**: a board posting absent from its board's list on two sweeps running is not proposed closed. The sweep stores each board's filtered postings through its commits and keeps no list of what a board listed, a SmartRecruiters board is read up to its newest 200, and the stored address must match the board's own spelling: a follow up with its own counter per posting.

## Testing strategy

- Unit: slug and duplicate rules; scoring port golden test against the engine on the real CSV; filter terms; never_apply rules; importer mapping of every CSV column and every file type.
- Integration (real Postgres and Redis): every endpoint, isolation (user B sees none of A's rows, files, sweeps), bulk in one transaction, concurrent edits, sweep against a fake website search (`api/tests/jobs/sweep_helpers.py`), one sweep per user at a time, idempotent import, applied files immutable, a scheduled slot enqueues once (daylight saving time included).
- End to end (Playwright, locally and on the Jetson): Harald's import shows 433 rows with the same status counts as the CSV; filters and URL state; bulk status change; detail with PDF preview; add by hand; a website search against fake pages locally; map pins; screenshots light and dark, desktop and 375px.

## Boundaries

- **Always:** user scoping on every query; keep privacy-safe events; respect consent for interactive search and the line of its browser search (no page pacing nor checkpoint pause there since 2026-11-04, [SPEC-browser, Interactive discovery](SPEC-browser.md#interactive-discovery-626)).
- **Ask first:** source-specific capabilities beyond this approved interactive contract; non-API support comes through the `browser` and `agents` modules.
- **Never:** change or delete a file of an `applied` row; read or collect site passwords or bypass per-person consent; log posting texts or files. A person's existing signed in browser is used only through the browser module's guarded profile and handoff path.

## Success criteria

1. Harald's 433 rows, their posting texts and their files are in the app on the Jetson, with status counts identical to the CSV, and the import is idempotent.
2. The scoring port matches the engine on every scorable row (golden test).
3. Explicit API sweeps add only new matching postings, scored; a user's schedule, once turned on, runs by itself. The model-driven Find jobs flow additionally requires the interactive search acceptance evidence above.
4. Filters, bulk actions, detail and map work on desktop and phone.
5. No user can see another user's jobs, files or sweeps (automated isolation tests).
